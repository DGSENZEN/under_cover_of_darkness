## What it is

The ward-tongue is the old carved language the gates are cut in: a ward is a sentence in stone that holds a threshold
shut to some and open to others. It is older than the burning script on the portal's rings (the city's written hand,
`tools/textures/library_glyphs.py`): that one is spoken and written fast; this one is cut, square and slow. A careful
eye can see the written hand's strokes descend from the carved forms.

It has no sounds written here: only meanings. (Words to be spoken are the user's to write.)

## How a glyph is built

Every glyph is a square cartouche cut into a stone's face, built the same way:

1. **Its frame: what kind of word it is.**
   - *Round*: a power or a name (who or what).
   - *Stepped* (each corner cut in steps): an act (what is done).
   - *Cut-cornered* (each corner cut off straight): a place or a threshold (where).
2. **Its root, at its heart: what it is about.** Ten roots:

   | # | Root | Its sign | Why |
   |---|------|----------|-----|
   | 0 | the dead | a closed eye (a ring, a line through it) | the eye that no longer opens |
   | 1 | the living | a breath scroll | breath leaving the mouth: speech, life |
   | 2 | the deep | three arcs opening downward | a well with no floor |
   | 3 | fire | three tongues rising | |
   | 4 | water | two waves | |
   | 5 | stone | a block with its core | what holds |
   | 6 | sight | an open eye, its pupil | |
   | 7 | passage | two footprints, one ahead | the old maps' sign for a journey |
   | 8 | binding | two bands crossed, knotted | |
   | 9 | the eclipse | the eaten sun: a dark disc, its ring, its rays | the seal every ward is bound in; only a keystone carries it |

3. **Its marks: how the word is turned.**
   - *Dots* along its foot: how much, how many (one, two: fast, three: all of them).
   - *A bar* across its root: not, none, closed.
   - *A hook* at its right edge: toward, into, through.

## How an arch reads

An arch's stones read as one ward from the foot of its left jamb up to its crown, and the same ward again from the
foot of its right jamb up to its crown: it reads the same from either side, so it holds whichever way it is
approached. Its keystone, at the crown, is the seal it is bound in: the eclipse, the only glyph that burns marigold.
An arch with more stones than the ward has words carries *here, on stone* again at its feet (its footing courses).

## The passages' ward (the draft)

Each half of each arch, from the foot up:

| Glyph | Frame | Root | Marks | Reads |
|-------|-------|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | cut-cornered | stone | | here, on stone |
| 2 | stepped | binding | two dots | bound fast |
| 3 | stepped | sight | bar | unseen |
| 4 | round | the living | bar | no living one |
| 5 | stepped | passage | | passes |
| 6 | cut-cornered | the deep | hook | into the deep |
| 7 | round | the dead | three dots | all the dead |
| 8 | stepped | passage | hook | go through |
| key | round | the eclipse | | by the eaten sun |

Read whole: *Here, on stone, bound fast and unseen: no living one passes into the deep; all the dead go through, by
the eaten sun.* It is why the walker feels the plane shift as they cross: a living one is passing where the ward says
none does.

An arch too small for the whole ward drops words from its feet first (the crown's words always stay).
