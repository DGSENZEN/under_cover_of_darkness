# The coast and the harbour's life: references (Oct 2 2026)

Gathered for the harbour's art pass (the user: better geometry for the nature,
a few more details to feel alive). Atlantic tide range 2-4 m (Leixoes springs
c.3.4 m, Lisbon c.4 m): every quay, step, cave and rock shows a 3-4 m wet band
below a dry one. Peniscola is Mediterranean (no tide): take its forms (tombolo,
bufador), not its waterline.

## Rocky shores (granite: Foz do Douro, Galicia; limestone: Cascais; schist: Costa Vicentina)

Ranked: (1) colour bands by height: grey/orange lichen on dry rock; a black
"tar" band of Verrucaria maura (under 1 m sheltered, 5 m+ on exposed
headlands, its foot c. high water); a grey-white barnacle crust; blue-black
mussels, goose barnacles in cracks; olive-brown wrack; pink-white coralline at
low water; everything below high water darker and glossy (at night the wet
specular band does most of the work). (2) Blocks shaped by joints, not noise:
two steep joint sets and curved sheet joints: stacked cuboids with rounded
tops, cracks every 0.5-3 m, slabs 0.3-2 m; blobby rock reads fake. (3) A notch
at the waterline: an undercut 0.5-2 m deep at high water, an overhang over it.
(4) Boulder fields at cliff feet (granite): 0.3-1.5 m, a few over 2 m, rounded
where waves reach, angular in fresh rockfall, half-buried in coarse sand,
black voids between. (5) Geos: slot inlets 1-5 m wide along a joint. (6) Ledges
along sheet joints or bedding, 0.3-2 m deep, plant tufts on top, white guano
streaks below. (7) Sea-cave mouths along a joint: tall slots or keyholes 3-15 m
high; a cobble floor, walls polished to the barnacle line, a dripping roof
(Boca do Inferno: a collapsed roof, a chasm with an arch, dark grey with
algae). (8) The blowhole (Peniscola's bufador): a cave 65 m into the rock from a
10 m mouth narrowing at once, ending in a 6 m shaft opening in the town; a
snorting roar; spray to 20 m in storms; wet puddled rock round the shaft.
(9) Rock pools 0.5-3 m, 10-50 cm deep, mirror-black; gnammas on top.
(10) Tafoni 0.2-1 m with a dark hard rim. (11) Schist: near-vertical ribs, a
sawtooth outline, platy scree, no boulders. (12) Limestone: pitted surfaces,
horizontal ledges, deeper overhangs.

## Coastal vegetation

Ranked: (1) sea fennel (Crithmum): spray-zone crevices just above the black
band and in harbour wall joints, grey-green clumps 30-60 cm; (2) thrift
(Armeria pubigera / maritima): cliff faces and clifftop turf, cushions 10-30 cm,
pink heads; (3) gorse (Ulex) and low heather: clifftops, pruned by the wind to
prostrate cushions 20-50 cm at the edge, 1-2 m inland (the size gradient sells
the wind); (4) broom (Cytisus): slopes behind the clifftop, 1-2 m, green rods;
(5) rock-rose (Cistus): dry sunny slopes, 0.5-2 m; (6) maritime pine: dunes and
the inland edge, 20-35 m, leaning from the sea, flattened crowns; (7) stone
pine: sheltered southern ground, 12-25 m umbrella; (8) tamarisk: salty river
banks and estuaries, 3-5 m, feathery grey-green (the river mouth); (9) fig: out
of wall joints and rock cracks, 3-10 m (one gnarled fig from a fort or quay
wall); (10) red valerian: old walls, 40-80 cm, pink-red; (11) ivy curtains on
shaded walls; (12) pellitory, navelwort and small ferns in joints and wall
feet. Period: no ice plant (Carpobrotus came late); prickly pear (1493) and
agave (mid-16th c., established by the 1700s) at most one each, in a southern
garden.

## Harbour life at night, 16th-17th century (ranked for night stealth)

No public street lighting in Lisbon until 1780-90: all light from windows,
doorways, shrines, carried lanterns and fires. (1) Lit windows and doorways,
shutters ajar, warm slits, spill on wet stone (openings c.1 x 1.4 m). (2) Wall
shrines with lamps (alminhas: niches c.40 x 60 cm, a tile panel, an iron grille,
candles, flowers, alms). (3) Moored boats that move: 4-8 m, tied to iron rings,
rocking and bumping, creaking; some hauled up a slipway on their sides; river
boats with casks (pipas c.550 L). (4) The quay wall and its tide: stairs into
the water, the lowest 3-4 m slick green weed and barnacles, iron rings 20-30 cm,
wooden ladders, rope fenders, posts; a few old cannon muzzle-down as bollards
(17th c.). (5) A tavern: a bush of laurel or pine over the door, spill, voices,
barrels, a dozing drunk. (6) Fishermen with lanterns, nets and baskets round
the slip (night sardine netting). (7) Brown bark-tanned nets on poles, walls
and boats, cork floats, a mending spot. (8) Fish-trade clutter: wicker baskets
50-70 cm, salted-sardine barrels, sacks, a handcart with its shafts down,
oars, a grapnel, coiled rope, a pitch kettle. (9) Animals: gulls roosting on
ridges and boats (startled: calls, a natural alarm), shags at the foot of the
cliffs, cats on walls (eye-shine), rats on the quay edge, a far dog. (10) Smoke
from chimneys against the moon; a brazier at the fort gate. (11) Wet surfaces:
puddles, drips from eaves, a wrack line on the slipway, rubbish in corners.
(12) Wind: pennants on masts and the fort. (13) Laundry between windows (moving
occluders). (14) A fountain (chafariz) with clay jugs.
