# Iberian references for the rock city (harbour to summit)

Research pack for the 90-minute night mission: a grand Iberian city on a rock above a river gorge that meets the sea, crowned by a castle. Spanish and Portuguese architecture; the English-style castle parts are researched separately. Compiled 2026-09-28. Read-only research: nothing was downloaded.

## How to read this

- **Dimensions** always carry a source link. "en.wiki", "es.wiki", "pt.wiki" = Wikipedia in that language; "official" = the monument's own site or a public body; "secondary" = a travel guide, blog or a search-engine summary.
- **(unverified)** = found only on a secondary site or in a search summary. **(conflict)** = sources disagree; all values are given. **(not found)** = I looked and found no reliable number. Nothing is estimated or invented.
- **Images**: Wikimedia Commons file pages and categories. Every link was checked to exist through the Commons API on 2026-09-28. I matched them to the monuments by their titles and descriptions and did not open the photos. Categories hold many photos, which is useful for sampling textures and colours.
- **Model it** = what a modeller must get right at our PS2-inspired detail (low-poly silhouettes, 128-256 px photo textures, baked vertex colour, fog). **Play it** = stealth and traversal affordances. Both are my design notes, built on the sourced facts above them.

## 0. Whole-city analogues (the skeleton)

The brief has five real models. Use them for the macro layout; the district entries below supply the building kits.

| Real place | Why it fits | Sourced facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Toledo** | Hill ringed on three sides by the Tagus in a gorge, crowned by the Alcázar at the highest point, entered by gates and two fortified bridges | The meander (the "Torno del Tajo") wraps three quarters of the hill; the valley turns into "a narrow gorge with an asymmetric V section" cut in granite and migmatite ([IGME](https://info.igme.es/ielig/LIGInfo.aspx?codigo=TM105)); the Alcázar is "located in the highest part of town" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain)) |
| **Porto** | Built "along the hillsides overlooking the mouth of the Douro" ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/755/)): the gorge meets the sea. Quay at the bottom, the fortress-like cathedral on top | The Douro runs "between the granite banks, where the Cathedral of Porto and the escarpment of the Serra do Pilar form a box valley" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Luís_I_Bridge)) |
| **Lisbon** | The Alfama labyrinth climbs from the Tagus to the Castelo de São Jorge; palace square on the water | Alfama survived the 1755 earthquake as "a labyrinth of narrow streets and small squares" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfama)) |
| **Ronda** | A town split by a gorge and stitched together by three bridges | Puente Nuevo 98 m high ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_Nuevo_(Ronda))) |
| **Peñíscola** | A walled town on a sea rock tied to the land by a sand spit, crowned by a Templar castle | Headland about 67 m high, joined to the mainland by a tombolo; castle 1307 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peñíscola)) |

**Design takeaway (my inference from the sources):** every real analogue stacks its districts vertically and links them in four ways: stairs and ramps, bridges, walled corridors (the *coracha*), and water. Our "several levels in one" can be wired through one water network, and every link has a real precedent:

- **Aqueduct to distribution house:** the Mãe d'Água's "Register House" controlled the water sent to fountains, factories, convents and noble houses ([official](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/water-museum-mae-d-agua-das-amoreiras-reservoir)).
- **Distribution house to palace:** the Acequia Real brings water from the Darro "about 6.1 km east of the Alhambra" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalife)).
- **Palace to cisterns and baths:** the Albaicín has at least 20 surviving cisterns ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albaicín)).
- **Down to the gorge and its mills:** Ronda's mill channels even pass through a bridge pier ([desfiladerodeltajo.info](https://desfiladerodeltajo.info/10-las-acequias-arabes-y-molinos-harineros-del-tajo/)).
- **River back up to the fortress:** Córdoba's Albolafia waterwheel lifted river water to the Alcázar ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albolafia)).

## 1. The harbour

### 1.1 Seville's river port: Torre del Oro, the chain and the Reales Atarazanas (BEST KIT)
**What and why:** a river port with a lime-rendered tower that anchored a chain across the river, a wall running to a second tower, and a huge brick shipyard. The shipyard later served as fish market, as the Crown's trade and customs house, and as warehouses. Every harbour prop the brief lists is here in one place.

**Dimensions**
- Torre del Oro: 36.75 m total; the first body is 15.20 m in diameter; built 1220-1221 (Almohad). About 5 m of lime-concrete slab foundation, now about 11 m of foundation in all ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_del_Oro)).
- Three bodies: a 12-sided base; a second body, also 12-sided, "of only 8 metres" (14th c., Peter of Castile); a round top body of 1760 after 1755 earthquake damage ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_del_Oro)).
- The render was "a mixture of mortar, lime and pressed hay", which gave a golden reflection on the river ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_del_Oro)).
- It was "one of two anchor points for a large chain that would have been able to block the river". A section of wall joined it to the Torre de la Plata, barring the Arenal district. In 1248 Ramón de Bonifaz broke the river defences ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_del_Oro)). It is an *albarrana* tower: detached from the wall and linked to it ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albarrana_tower)).
- Reales Atarazanas: ordered in 1252 by Alfonso X, with 10 galleys built by 1253. It had 15 naves, later extended to 17, covering about 15,000 m². Brick groin vaults sit on "thick, slightly pointed arches"; only 7 of the 17 naves survive, after 5 were demolished in 1945 ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reales_Atarazanas_de_Sevilla)).
- Later uses: fish market (1493); the **Casa de la Contratación**, the Crown's trade house for the Indies, was first housed there (1503); five naves became artillery stores (1719). A sandy flat, the Resolana, ran down to the river ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reales_Atarazanas_de_Sevilla)).
- In November 2016 the Atarazanas were the Red Keep dungeons in *Game of Thrones* ([Seville provincial tourism, official](https://www.turismosevilla.org/en/what-see-and-do/recommended-plans/game-thrones-sets)).

**Model it**
- Torre del Oro is three stacked prisms: a crenellated 12-sided base, a smaller 12-sided stage, then a cylinder with a small dome. It is a cheap landmark whose identity is the warm golden render colour. Keep 12 facets; don't round it off.
- Atarazanas: build one nave bay (two brick piers, a slightly pointed transverse arch and a groin vault) and array it 7-17 times. Use a tiling 256 px brick texture and darken the vault crowns and pier bases in vertex colour.
- Chain: a winch in the tower; the chain is a low-segment mesh or an alpha strip across the water to a second anchor tower on the far bank.

**Play it**
- The harbour chain is a gate puzzle: raise or lower it. A slack chain is a grab-line across the water at night.
- The wall-walk from the albarrana tower to its partner tower is a patrol route and a player route.
- The naves give deep, repetitive dark bays for stealth. The Contratación is the customs house: ledgers, seals and seized cargo make objectives and loot.

**Images:** [Torre del Oro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torre_del_Oro_flag_Seville_Spain.jpg) · [Atarazanas naves](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Atarazanas_de_Sevilla_02.jpg) · [Category: Torre del Oro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Torre_del_Oro)

### 1.2 Porto's Ribeira and the Guindais stairs (BEST TOPOGRAPHY)
**What and why:** the exact "gorge meets the sea" condition. Tall, narrow tiled houses stack above the quay, and stairs and the old wall climb to the fortress-like cathedral.

**Dimensions**
- Escadas dos Guindais: link the Ribeira quay to the upper town (Batalha). Of 12th-c. origin, rebuilt in the 19th c., about 290 steps, beside remnants of the 14th-c. Fernandine wall (secondary, unverified).
- Dom Luís I bridge: 1881-1886 and iron, so the wrong period, but it shows the gorge's scale. Arch span 172 m; upper deck 395.25 m long ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Luís_I_Bridge)).
- Porto Cathedral "gives the impression of a fortified church". It is flanked by two square towers, each with two buttresses, and has a rose window under a crenellated arch; the cloister has baroque azulejos of 1729-1731 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Cathedral)). Porto is called "the granite city" (secondary).
- House heights and quay dimensions: (not found).

**Model it**
- Facade strips of narrow houses, each one extruded box. A tiled or coloured-render texture sits inside a granite-frame texture around the openings; iron balconies are alpha cards. Get variation from colour, not geometry.
- A heavy, low granite arcade runs along the quay with the houses above it.

**Play it**
- Balconies ladder up the facades. The stairs are an alternative vertical route to the lanes. The quay arcade is a shadowed walkway, and the river edge is a drop-into-water escape.

**Images:** [Cais da Ribeira](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cais_da_Ribeira_in_Porto_%287%29.jpg) · [Escadas dos Guindais](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Escadas_dos_Guindais_Porto_%288022699637%29.jpg) · [Category: Ribeira (Porto)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Ribeira_%28Porto%29)

### 1.3 Drassanes Reials, Barcelona (the covered royal shipyard)
**What and why:** a walled Gothic shipyard of long parallel naves, big enough to build galleys indoors.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drassanes_Reials_de_Barcelona))**
- After the 1381 works: eight naves, 8.4 m high and 8.4 m wide, about 60 m long, "consisting of 17 columns 77 cm wide and 6 m high".
- A "rectangular fortification with no roof consisting of fortified walls and four towers", two of which survive.
- Twelve galleys were built at once in 1423; the *Real*, flagship at Lepanto, was built there in 1571. Ashlar came from Montjuïc; ropes and tiles came from Valencia.

**Model it:** long nave halls: one bay module with the pier, the arch and a roof section, arrayed. Long sightlines inside, so break them up with timber stacks and a hull on the stocks.

**Play it:** a half-built ship as a set piece (climb the ribs and scaffolding). The yard's corner towers and walls make the harbour's inner fortress.

**Images:** [Drassanes](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Drassanes_de_Barcelona.jpg) · [Category: Drassanes Reials](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Drassanes_Reials_de_Barcelona)

### 1.4 Llotja de Palma (the customs house and merchants' exchange)
**What and why:** a Gothic merchants' hall on the old seafront, a jewel-box "customs house" with a strong silhouette.

**Dimensions and features**
- Built 1426-1452 by Guillem Sagrera. Inside are four naves of equal height on six helical (twisted) columns with no base or capital ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja_de_Palma_de_Mallorca)).
- Twelve cross-ribbed vaults and eight large tracery windows; four octagonal corner towers plus ten smaller tower-buttresses (secondary, via search summary).
- The guardian angel over the portal holds a band reading "defenedor de la Mercancía" ("defender of merchandise"); gargoyles; built in the Raval de Mar on the old seafront; Santanyí stone for the columns ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja_de_Palma_de_Mallorca)).
- Hall length, width and height: (not found).

**Model it:** a rectangular box with four octagonal corner turrets, small turret-buttresses along the sides and a crenellated parapet. Inside, the six twisted columns (8-12 sides, with the spiral carried by texture) run their ribs straight into the vault: the "palm grove" effect.

**Play it:** the corner turrets hold stairs to the battlemented roof walk. The hall is the customs vault.

**Images:** [Llotja exterior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lonja,_Palma_de_Mallorca,_España,_2022-10-06,_DD_22-24_HDR.jpg) · [Category: Llotja de Palma](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Llotja_de_Palma)

### 1.5 Torre de Belém, Lisbon (the river-mouth fort)
**What and why:** the Manueline fort that guards the Tagus mouth: a tower on a bastion in the water.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belém_Tower))**
- 30 m tall, about 12 m wide, four storeys.
- An "irregular, hexagonal bastion, with elongated flanks", with embrasures for 17 cannons.
- Built of lioz limestone, c. 1514-1519, by Francisco de Arruda.
- Decoration: ropes, armillary spheres and crosses of the Order of Christ. The corner watch-turrets (*guerites*) are cylindrical with domes and zoomorphic corbels, one of them a rhinoceros.

**Model it:** a square tower on a projecting bastion platform at water level; domed guerites (8-12-sided cylinders) on the corners. The twisted-rope moulding is a thin band texture; add a loggia and balconies facing the river.

**Play it:** the cannon embrasures at water level are the boat entrance. Loggia balconies are a climb route. It is a natural signal post for the harbour chain.

**Images:** [Torre de Belém](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torre_Belém_April_2009-4a.jpg) · [Category: Torre de Belém](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Torre_de_Belém)

### 1.6 Praça do Comércio, Lisbon (the palace square on the water)
**What and why:** Lisbon's riverside royal square: arcaded on three sides and open to the water, on the site of the palace destroyed in 1755. The model for a palace-on-the-quay set piece.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praça_do_Comércio))**
- 175 x 175 m (30,600 m²), U-shaped and open to the Tagus.
- "Galleries on their ground floors"; the arms end in two large towers.
- Site of the Ribeira royal palace, destroyed in 1755. A grand marble staircase at the water's edge received arriving dignitaries.

**Model it:** one arcade-bay module on three sides; the square itself is a big open plane with fog.

**Play it:** a lamplit 175 m square is a no-go zone, and the arcades are the stealth route around it. The water stairs are a boat landing and an escape.

**Images:** [Praça do Comércio](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Empty_Praça_do_Comércio_during_the_COVID-19_lockdown,_Lisbon,_Portugal_julesvernex2.jpg) · [Category: Praça do Comércio](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Praça_do_Comércio)

### 1.7 The Nasrid shipyard gate, Málaga (the SEA GATE)
**What and why:** the surviving 14th-c. gate of Málaga's Nasrid shipyard, a real sea gate in marble and jasper, now stranded inland.

**Dimensions and features ([Málaga city archaeological routes, official](https://rutasarqueologicas.malaga.eu/punto-n-20/))**
- 14th c., "probably built by Yusuf I (1318-1354)": "a pointed horseshoe arch built with marble ashlars and jaspon", topped by a lintel framed with an *alfiz*.
- The alfiz holds two diagonal shields with Arabic inscriptions.
- The shipyard behind it had "5 longitudinal naves that would open towards the south shore through 2 doors", crossed by 10 perpendicular naves, about 5,008 m².
- Reused as the entrance of the central market in 1880. It was one of the gates to the sea in the medieval walls (secondary).

**Model it:** one monumental gate slab: a pointed horseshoe arch, the alfiz and two shields. One mesh and one 256 px texture give a strong silhouette over water.

**Play it:** a water gate that boats row through into a covered dock; a portcullis or chain, with a guard post above.

**Images:** [Nasrid gate](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Puerta_nazarí_de_Atarazanas.jpg) · [Gate, 2019](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Málaga_-_Puerta_de_las_Atarazanas_2019-10-21_01.jpg)

### 1.8 Peñíscola (the sea-rock variant)
**What and why:** a walled town on a sea rock tied to the land by a sand spit, with a sea cave: the harbour's rock-and-cave variant.

**Features:** a rock headland about 67 m high on a tombolo; castle built 1307 by the Templars. The Bufador is a sea cave "with a landward entrance through which the seawater escapes in clouds of spray" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peñíscola)). It was Meereen in *Game of Thrones* season 6 (secondary).

**Play it:** the blowhole is a smugglers' sea cave; its roar and spray cover sound and sight.

**Images:** [Peñíscola from the beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Peniscola_from_the_beach._Year_1985.jpg) · [Castle crowning the town](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castillo-Palacio_de_Peñíscola_%28castellón%29.jpg)

## 2. The old town

### 2.1 Alfama, Lisbon (BEST)
**What and why:** the steep lanes, stairs, small squares, azulejos and viewpoints of the brief, climbing from the river to the castle.

**Features ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfama))**
- Survived the 1755 earthquake and "has remained a labyrinth of narrow streets and small squares".
- The Miradouro de Santa Luzia sits "over remnants of the Moorish city walls"; the Miradouro das Portas do Sol is nearby.
- The name comes from Arabic *al-ḥamma*, "hot springs"; the Chafariz d'El-Rei fountain dates from c. 1570-80.
- Street widths and stair counts: (not found).

**Model it**
- Houses are rendered boxes (whitewash or pastel render) with stone door and window frames and tile panels, under low-pitched terracotta canal-tile roofs. Build 6-10 house modules with 2-3 texture variants each, and mirror and rotate them.
- Stairs (*escadinhas*) with iron handrails. The miradouro is a terrace with a pergola, azulejo benches and wall panels.
- Laundry lines are alpha-card sheets strung between facades.

**Play it**
- Miradouros are exposed lamplit terraces with a vista of the castle: danger plus orientation.
- Fountains in the small squares mask noise; stairs are shortcuts; balconies and laundry lines give cover and crossings between facades. The roofs are a second layer of the maze.

**Images:** [Alfama](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lisbon_alfalma.jpg) · [Small square and stairway](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lisboa,_Alfama_24.jpg) · [Santa Luzia azulejos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lisboa,_Miradouro_de_Santa_Luzia,_azulejos_%281%29.jpg)

### 2.2 Albaicín, Granada (whitewash, walled gardens, cisterns)
**What and why:** the Moorish quarter facing the Alhambra: whitewashed walled gardens and a Nasrid street plan, with cisterns under the lanes.

**Features ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albaicín))**
- Keeps its Nasrid-period (13th-15th c.) street plan.
- *Cármenes*: "a freestanding house with typically whitewashed walls and including a small orchard or garden".
- "At least twenty underground cisterns still exist": the Aljibe del Rey holds 300 m³ (11th c.), the Aljibe de Trillo 50 m³ (14th c.).
- The walled area is about 75 ha (Zirid and Nasrid walls; the Puerta de Elvira, the Arco de las Pesas and the Puerta Monaita). The Mirador de San Nicolás faces the Alhambra.

**Model it:** long blank white garden walls with few openings, with cypress and vine tops showing above. The walls are the silhouette, cheap and very readable under moonlight.

**Play it:** garden walls form a climb-and-leap network, and private gardens are safe pockets. The cisterns are underground links and hiding places. The mirador is a public viewpoint with watchmen.

**Images:** [Albaicín panorama](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Albayzín_panorama_%282010%29.jpg) · [Street of cármenes](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Granada-Albaicín-Calle_de_Zarfa-20110920.jpg) · [View from the Mirador de San Nicolás](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Granada_-_View_from_Mirador_de_San_Nicolás_-_02.jpg)

### 2.3 Toledo (the rock city: covered passages and banded walls)
**What and why:** the closest real match to the whole brief (rock, gorge, a crowning Alcázar), with two street and wall devices worth stealing.

**Features**
- A hill surrounded on three sides by the Tagus. Gates: Bisagra, Sol, Cambrón, Bab al-Mardum; bridges: Alcántara and San Martín ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain)).
- **Cobertizos:** raised covered passages that link buildings over the streets. They let nobles and enclosed religious communities (for example, cloistered nuns) cross between buildings without going outside. The Cobertizo de Santo Domingo el Real is the longest surviving (secondary: [Pasearte Toledo](https://www.paseartetoledo.es/blog/los-cobertizos-de-toledo/), via search summary).
- **Aparejo toledano:** a mixed wall of brick courses framing rubble-stone panels (*cajones*), with brick "chains" at the corners. The number of brick courses varies; it was common in Castilian building from the 10th to the 15th c. ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparejo_toledano)).

**Model it:** aparejo toledano is a perfect PS2 texture: one 256 px tile of horizontal brick bands and stone panels instantly reads as "Toledo". A cobertizo is a box bridging a lane with a dark barrel vault underneath.

**Play it:** cobertizos are overhead bridges between buildings, so the player can cross above patrols, and they make dark tunnels at street level.

**Images:** [Toledo and the Tagus](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Toledo_%2837737041515%29.jpg) · [A cobertizo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cobertizo_del_Colegio_de_Doncellas._Toledo.jpg) · [Aparejo toledano (La Pueblanueva)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Pueblanueva_%282023%29_11.jpg)

### 2.4 Cáceres old town (stone tower-houses; King's Landing)
**What and why:** a walled granite old town of noble tower-houses, already used as King's Landing; the stone counterpart to the whitewashed lanes.

**Features ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_of_Cáceres))**
- "Thirty towers from the Islamic period still stand", the Torre del Bujaco the most famous.
- Protected area 9 ha, buffer zone 60.63 ha; UNESCO 1986.
- Queen Isabella ordered the houses that had backed the rival claimant to lower their towers; the Palacio de las Cigüeñas kept its tower.
- *Game of Thrones* season 7 used the Arco de la Estrella and the Plaza Santa María for King's Landing.

**Model it:** granite ashlar lower storeys, heraldic shields over doors, and square towers with flat (cut-down) or crenellated tops. With few windows, the masses stay blocky and strong. Use a warm granite texture with lichen tints.

**Play it:** tower-houses are vertical hideouts and overwatch points; cut-down towers are flat roof platforms. The Arco de la Estrella gate is a choke point.

**Images:** [Old town of Cáceres](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Parte_antigua_de_Cáceres,_Extremadura,_España.jpg)

### 2.5 Arcos de la Frontera and the white towns (whitewash on a cliff)
**What and why:** the Andalusian white town at its most dramatic, stacked on a cliff over a loop of the river.

**Features**
- The town sits "atop a sandstone ridge"; part of the old town lies inside "a sharp loop of the river" at the foot of "towering vertical cliffs". Elevation 185 m; the castle was rebuilt in the first half of the 15th c.; typical *ventanas con orejeras* ("windows with earmuffs") ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcos_de_la_Frontera)).
- The white towns share "whitewashed walls and red or brown tiled roofs"; whitewashing became predominant during the plague waves from the 14th c. on ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Towns_of_Andalusia)).

**Model it:** white boxes flush with the cliff edge; the sandstone cliff is one big textured mesh.

**Play it:** edge houses are ledges over the void; the cliff-top balcony is an exposed viewpoint.

**Images:** [Arcos de la Frontera](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arcos_de_la_Frontera_-_001_%2830591512202%29.jpg) · [Category: Arcos de la Frontera](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Arcos_de_la_Frontera)

## 3. The aqueduct

### 3.1 Águas Livres aqueduct and the Mãe d'Água reservoir, Lisbon (BEST: valley crossing and distribution house)
**What and why:** the tallest stone arches in the set, crossing a valley into the city and ending at a vaulted reservoir that shared the water out. This is the brief's aqueduct-to-distribution-house chain, for real.

**Dimensions**
- The main aqueduct runs about 14.2 km; the whole network of canals and galleries nearly 58 km ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Águas_Livres_Aqueduct)).
- The Alcântara valley crossing is 941 m long with 35 arches: 21 semicircular and 14 pointed.
- The Arco Grande "reaches 65 metres in height with a span of nearly 29 metres" (a pointed masonry arch).
- In operation from 1748, carrying about 1,300 m³ a day. The public walkway on top closed in 1852; contrary to legend, not because of the murderer Diogo Alves ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Águas_Livres_Aqueduct)).
- Mãe d'Água das Amoreiras, the reservoir and distribution house: designed by Carlos Mardel (1745/1746), completed 1834, on a raised platform with an austere exterior ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mãe_d'Água_das_Amoreiras)).
- Its tank is 7.5 m deep, with a panoramic terrace on top. "On the western front of the reservoir is the Register House, where water flows driven to fountains, factories, convents and noble houses were controlled" ([official](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/water-museum-mae-d-agua-das-amoreiras-reservoir)). Capacity about 5,500 m³ ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Águas_Livres_Aqueduct)).
- A cascade inside "was built with stone from the springs that feed the reservoir" ([Trienal de Lisboa](https://www.trienaldelisboa.com/ohl/espaco/reservatorio-da-mae-d-agua-das-amoreiras-museu-da-agua/); [pt.wiki](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservatório_da_Mãe_d'Água_das_Amoreiras)). A dolphin-mouth spout and four columns rising from the tank to carry rib vaults: (unverified, secondary).

**Model it**
- The arches get taller toward the valley centre, so build 3-4 pier-and-arch modules at different heights rather than one. The pointed central arches (the Arco Grande among them) are the silhouette: 65 m pointed arches in fog make the city's most memorable skyline element after the castle.
- The reservoir is a plain stone cube on a platform. Inside: the tank, the vaults and the cascade wall.

**Play it**
- The walkway on top is a long, exposed "sky bridge" into the palace district, with guard posts at the towers.
- The Register House valves are a puzzle: cut the water to a fountain to silence it, or drain a cistern or tunnel elsewhere (see 7.5). The 7.5 m tank is a dive or drop; the roof terrace is a lookout.

**Images:** [Águas Livres arcade](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aqueduto_das_Águas_Livres2.JPG) · [Mãe d'Água interior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mãe_d'Água_das_Amoreiras_%28Interior%29.JPG) · [Mãe d'Água exterior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Museu_da_Água.JPG)

### 3.2 Aqueduct of Segovia (the two-tier module)
**What and why:** the iconic two-tier Roman arcade running through a city square, and the cleanest repeatable module in the set.

**Dimensions**
- At most 28.5 m tall, "including nearly 6 m of foundation" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_of_Segovia)).
- 167 arches: 75 single arches, then 44 double arches (88 counted singly), then 4 single ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_of_Segovia)).
- Upper arches 5.10 m wide, lower arches about 4.50 m; built of granite ashlars laid without mortar ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acueducto_de_Segovia)).
- Piers are 1.8 x 2.5 m at the top and 2.4 x 3 m at the base; 36 semicircular arches were rebuilt in the 15th c. ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_of_Segovia)).
- Arcade length (conflict): es.wiki gives 813 m on a 1% slope to the Plaza del Azoguejo; en.wiki mentions a 728 m run on a 1% grade.
- Channel on top (conflict): a "U-shaped hollow measuring 0.55 tall by 0.46 metre diameter" (en.wiki) versus "180 x 150 cm" (es.wiki).
- The system before the arcade: El Caserón, a cistern for spring water 17 km from the city; then a channel of ashlars to a second tower, the Casa de Aguas, where the water settles and drops its sand ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acueducto_de_Segovia)).

**Model it:** the ideal PS2 module. One double-arch bay (tall lower arch, upper arch, piers that shrink in steps) instanced along a spline. The stepped taper must be geometry; the rough ashlar pattern is a 256 px texture. Darken the lower piers in vertex colour.

**Play it:** if the en.wiki channel figure is right, the channel is not a walkway: you balance on the top edge. There is no floor between the tiers, so the upper arcade is a balance-beam route. The Casa de Aguas is a small settling-tank room.

**Images:** [Segovia aqueduct](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aqueduct_of_Segovia_08.jpg) · [Category: Aqueduct of Segovia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Aqueduct_of_Segovia)

### 3.3 Acueducto de los Milagros, Mérida (the striped ruin)
**What and why:** a ruined Roman aqueduct of striped granite and brick: the reference for texture and broken spans.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acueducto_de_los_Milagros))**
- "Thirty-eight arched pillars standing 25 metres high along a course of some 830 metres".
- *Opus mixtum*: "granite ashlar blocks interspersed with red brick".
- Fed from the Proserpina dam about 5 km away; 1st c. AD, with a second phase around 300 AD.

**Model it:** the granite-and-brick banding is texture only; missing arches give silhouette variety cheaply.

**Play it:** broken spans make jump-and-gap puzzles: a ruined old branch next to the working aqueduct.

**Images:** [Los Milagros](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_acueducto_de_Los_Milagros.jpg) · [Category: Acueducto de los Milagros](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Acueducto_de_los_Milagros)

### 3.4 Pegões aqueduct, Tomar (arches on arches)
**What and why:** a Renaissance aqueduct feeding a Templar convent, with a row of round arches stacked on tall pointed ones.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegões_Aqueduct))**
- About 6 km long; the article gives both 6,223 and 6,233 m.
- 180 arches cover about 400 m; over the Pegões valley, "58 rounded arches resting on 16 pointed arches".
- The ends differ in height by 26.28 m.
- Designed in 1584 by Filippo Terzi, built 1600-1614; water reached the main cloister in 1619. Maximum height: (not found).

**Model it:** tall pointed arches below carrying a row of smaller round arches above. The contrast between the two arch shapes is the signature.

**Images:** [Pegões aqueduct](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tomar_December_2008-4.jpg) · [Category: Aqueduto dos Pegões](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Aqueduto_dos_Pegões)

### 3.5 Amoreira aqueduct, Elvas (four tiers)
**What and why:** a fortress town's great aqueduct, stacked up to four tiers where the ground drops.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoreira_Aqueduct))**
- 7,054 m from the spring to the Chafariz do Jardim, plus 450 m to the town fountain.
- 1,367 m run underground, 4,049 m at ground level and 1,683 m on arcades.
- 833 arches, "with at most four registers at any one time". Rectangular pillars carry semi-circular and pyramidal buttresses.
- Built 1529-1622.

**Model it and play it:** the four-tier section is the tallest stack. The buttresses on the piers give climbing holds.

**Images:** [Amoreira aqueduct](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2020-08-23_Aqueduto_Amoreira_%286%29.jpg) · [Category: Aqueduto da Amoreira](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Aqueduto_da_Amoreira)

### 3.6 Água de Prata aqueduct, Évora (the aqueduct that becomes a street)
**What and why:** an aqueduct whose last arches enter the town and fill with houses and shops.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Água_de_Prata_Aqueduct))**
- About 18 km; begun 1531-1532 and built in about 6 years; Francisco de Arruda.
- Near its end, around Rua do Cano, "shops and cafés beneath and beside the arches"; water arrived at fountains including the Praça do Giraldo.

**Model it:** arches infilled with house fronts, doors and windows set inside the openings. Reuse the house modules at arch size.

**Play it:** the aqueduct top is a raised road over a residential lane, and the homes inside the arches are small interiors.

**Images:** [Água de Prata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Agua_de_Prata_Aqueduct_%2850866672371%29.jpg)

### 3.7 The Acequia Real (the channel into the palace)
**What and why:** the open channel that carried a river to the Alhambra: the link between aqueduct and palace.

**Features:** it draws water from the Darro "about 6.1 kilometers east of the Alhambra"; the Albercones reservoir could hold 400 m³; a well 17.4 m deep ([en.wiki Generalife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalife)). "The Sultan's Canal... brought water from the mountains to the east" ([en.wiki Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra)).

**Play it:** an open channel along the terraces. "Follow the water in" is a natural route, and the channel's noise masks footsteps.

## 4. The palace of waters

### 4.1 Alhambra, Nasrid palaces (BEST)
**What and why:** the benchmark palace of water: pools, channels and fountains in courtyards wrapped in plain red fortress walls.

**Dimensions**
- Whole Alhambra: about 700-740 m long, 200-205 m wide, about 142,000 m² ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra)).
- Built of red earth: "a granular aggregate held together by a medium of red clay", the root of the name "the red". "Buildings were designed to be seen from within and so their decoration was focused on the inside." "Tilework was generally used for lower walls or for floors, while stucco was used for upper zones", under carved wooden ceilings and muqarnas ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra)).
- Court of the Myrtles: 36.6 m long, 23-23.5 m wide; the pool is 34 x 7.10 m. The porticos at both ends have "a wide central arch flanked by three smaller arches on either side" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Myrtles)).
- Comares Tower: about 16 m wide and 45 m tall, walls 2-3 m thick ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Myrtles)).
- The Hall of the Ambassadors inside it is 11.3 m square and 18.2 m high; its wooden dome covers about 125 m² with 8,017 pieces in a twelve-pointed-star pattern ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Myrtles)).
- Court of the Lions: 28.7 x 15.6 m. "Single columns alternate with groups of two or three", in 17 bays on the long sides and 11 on the short. Pavilions project from the east and west porticos ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Lions)).
- The fountain is a marble basin on twelve lions of Macael marble; four water channels meet at it ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Lions)).
- Hall of the Two Sisters: a muqarnas dome of "at least 5000" pieces unfolding into sixteen miniature domes ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Lions)).

**Model it**
- **Exterior:** plain red-earth boxes and towers with few openings, which makes cheap, strong silhouettes. **Interior:** that is where the ornament budget goes.
- Every wall is three texture bands: a geometric tile dado below, a carved stucco field with epigraphic bands, then a wooden or muqarnas ceiling. Only the arches and column shafts are geometry.
- Columns are slender marble shafts. Get the Lions' 1-2-3 cluster rhythm right rather than the carving.
- Muqarnas: a stepped dome or cone of 3-5 rings of faceted cells, with ambient occlusion baked per ring in vertex colour; the texture adds the cell edges.
- Pools: a dark, glossy vertex-coloured plane with a baked or mirrored-geometry reflection of the tower. The pool is the composition.

**Play it**
- Pools are exposure (ripples, splashing), but the myrtle hedges run along both sides of the pool as waist-height cover.
- Porticos are bands of shadow. The Comares Tower is the vertical objective (the throne room). The projecting pavilions of the Lions court have climbable roofs.
- The four floor channels are noise hazards and carry small floating lures.
- Miradors (projecting lookout rooms, such as the Mirador de Lindaraja, or the Partal's Torre de las Damas over its pool) are lookouts for guards and players.

### 4.2 Generalife (terraced water gardens)
**What and why:** the Alhambra's summer villa above it: terraced gardens organised around a long water channel, plus a stairway with water running down its parapets.

**Dimensions**
- Patio de la Acequia 12.8 x 48.7 m; the mirador chamber is 3.98 m square; three hillside terraces each about 35 x 250 m ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalife)).
- Built late 13th c. (Muhammad II), redecorated 1319 (Isma'il I); major fire in 1958 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalife)).
- **Water Stairway:** "Water from the Royal Canal (Acequia Real) continues to flow down inverted pan tiles along the stairway parapets", under a dome of laurels. It leads to "the highest part of the Generalife" ([Patronato de la Alhambra, official](https://www.alhambra-patronato.es/en/edificios-lugares/the-water-stairway)).

**Model it:** a long, narrow court with a central channel, arcing jets as alpha particles and arcaded pavilions at both ends. The stair parapets are just half-pipe channels: cheap and memorable.

**Play it:** the flowerbeds beside the channel are cover, and running water masks footsteps. The Water Stairway is a sound-covered climb to the upper gardens.

**Images:** [Patio de la Acequia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Patio_de_la_Acequia_%28Generalife%29_-_DSC07863_%28slightly_cropped_and_sharpened%29.jpg) · [Category: Generalife](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Generalife)

### 4.3 Real Alcázar of Seville (the Mudéjar palace; Game of Thrones' Dorne)
**What and why:** a working royal palace of Mudéjar courts, sunken gardens, raised galleries and cisterns; Game of Thrones' Dorne.

**Dimensions and features**
- Grew from a roughly square enclosure about 100 m a side (913-914), plus an 11th-c. southern enclosure of about 70 x 80 m; six more enclosures from 1169 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcázar_of_Seville)).
- Patio de las Doncellas: its garden and pool were built 1356-1366, with "two flowerbeds located one meter beneath the pavement" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcázar_of_Seville)).
- *Game of Thrones* (from season 5), the Water Gardens of Dorne: filmed in the Grotto Gallery above the Mercury Pond, the Baths of Doña María de Padilla, the Dance Garden, the Carlos V and Lion pavilions, and the Hall of Ambassadors ([Seville provincial tourism, official](https://www.turismosevilla.org/en/what-see-and-do/recommended-plans/game-thrones-sets)).
- Baths of María de Padilla: a vaulted rainwater cistern of arches and still water (secondary); photos show a long pool under ribbed vaults. Dimensions: (not found).

**Model it:** the sunken garden, with beds a metre below the walkway, is a strong and cheap shape. Mudéjar lobed arches carry stucco panels (texture).

**Play it:** the sunken beds hide you below guards' eye lines. The raised Grotto Gallery is a wall-top walk over the gardens. The María de Padilla cistern is the underground way between palace and gardens.

**Images:** [Patio de las Doncellas](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Patio_de_las_Doncellas_%28Alcázar_de_Sevilla%29.jpg) · [Baths of María de Padilla](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alcázar_Baños_María_de_Padilla.jpg) · [Category: Alcázar of Seville](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Alcázar_of_Seville)

### 4.4 Sintra National Palace (Mudéjar and Manueline; the landmark chimneys)
**What and why:** a whitewashed royal palace of Moorish-tiled rooms, crowned by two giant conical kitchen chimneys.

**Dimensions and features**
- "The emblematic scale and shape of the two 33 metre chimneys became a landmark of Sintra"; the kitchen was built by King João I ([Parques de Sintra, official](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/national-palace-of-sintra/points-of-interest/kitchen/)).
- Swan Room (30 swans); Coat of Arms Room (72 coats of arms, 1515-1518); the Manueline campaign ran 1497-1530; Mudéjar-style tiles came from Seville ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintra_National_Palace)).

**Model it:** two white truncated cones (8-12 sides) rising from a plain whitewashed block. It is the cheapest landmark in the whole set.

**Play it:** the chimneys are a skyline beacon (smoke at night). The kitchen is a service entrance into the palace.

**Images:** [Sintra National Palace](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Palacio_Nacional,_Sintra,_Portugal,_2019-05-25,_DD_89.jpg) · [Category: Palácio Nacional de Sintra](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Palácio_Nacional_de_Sintra)

### 4.5 Aljafería, Zaragoza (interlaced polylobed arches; the moated palace)
**What and why:** an 11th-c. Islamic palace inside a moated fortress; the best reference for lace-like interlaced arches.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljafería))**
- Main palace mostly 1065-1081.
- The Golden Hall is 20 m long and 8 m wide under "thirty large, deep square coffers".
- "A twenty metre wide moat crossed by two drawbridges".
- The Troubadour Tower has five levels (late 9th c.); the oratory's chamfered corners make a false octagon.

**Model it:** interlaced polylobed and mixtilinear arches are flat panels with alpha cut-outs, which read as lace at a distance.

**Images:** [Aljafería](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aljafería_Palace.jpg) · [Category: Aljafería](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Aljafería)

### 4.6 Bath houses: El Bañuelo (Granada) and the Arab Baths of Ronda
**What and why:** the two best-preserved Moorish bathhouses, one of them at the foot of Ronda's gorge and fed by a waterwheel: the palace's bath kit.

**Features**
- El Bañuelo: cold, warm and hot rooms. "The ceilings are pierced with small star-shaped and octagonal skylights." Marble columns carry horseshoe arches, with capitals reused from Roman and Caliphal buildings; heated floors; on the Darro bank; traditionally dated to the 11th c. ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Bañuelo)).
- Ronda baths: cold, warm and hot rooms; a reception hall with "a large dome on cruciform pillars and lowered arches" ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baños_árabes_de_Ronda)).
- A waterwheel at the meeting of the Arroyo de las Culebras and the Guadalevín fed the Ronda baths through a small aqueduct. They stand at the foot of the vanished Puerta de la Puente, in the lower town by the river ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baños_árabes_de_Ronda)).

**Model it:** barrel vaults pierced by star-shaped holes that throw pools of moonlight onto the floor (vertex colour or projected light). Very cheap and very atmospheric.

**Play it:** steam is vision cover (a fog volume); the skylights are peepholes and drop points. The waterwheel is a noisy machine to start or stop.

**Images:** [El Bañuelo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Bañuelo._Sala_central.jpg) · [Ronda Arab baths](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Baños_Arabes3.jpg) · [Category: Baños árabes de Ronda](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Baños_árabes_de_Ronda)

## 5. The cathedral

### 5.1 Seville Cathedral, the Giralda and the Patio de los Naranjos (BEST)
**What and why:** the largest Gothic cathedral built over a mosque, keeping the minaret (the Giralda) and the orange-tree court; its roofs are walkable.

**Dimensions**
- Cathedral: 135 m long, 100 m wide; the Gothic body is 126 x 76 m. The central nave is 36 m high, 40 m at the crossing; 80 chapels ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_Cathedral)).
- Giralda base and shaft: base a square of 13.5 m ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giralda)) or 13.6 m ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giralda)). The Almohad shaft is 50.85 m (es.wiki) or 50.51 m (en.wiki).
- Giralda interior: "a series of 35 ramps winding around the perimeter of seven vaulted chambers", sized for "beasts of burden, people, and the custodians" (en.wiki). A final flight of 17 steps reaches the bells; 24 bells (es.wiki).
- Minaret completed 1198; belfry 1558-1568 (en.wiki). The Giraldillo statue is 3.5 m, 7.5 m with its pedestal (es.wiki); en.wiki gives "4 meters tall and approximately 4 meters wide".
- Giralda total height (conflict): 94.69 m (es.wiki); "approximately 95 or 96 meters" (en.wiki Giralda); 97.5 m (common secondary figure); 104.5 m to the weathervane (en.wiki Seville Cathedral).
- Giralda decoration: the faces carry "a network of lozenges" of brick arches (*sebka*) and paired windows with round or polylobed horseshoe arches ([Catedral de Sevilla, official](https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/the-cathedral/building/the-giralda/)). It reuses 92 Umayyad capitals (en.wiki).
- Patio de los Naranjos: "43 by 81 metres", built 1172-1186 as the mosque court; the fountain's upper basin is Visigothic; entered through the Puerta del Perdón ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patio_de_los_Naranjos_(Sevilla))).
- Roofs: the official "visit to the roofs" lasts about 1.5 hours: "stroll along its rooftops, wander through its passages", mostly outdoors ([official](https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/cultural-visit/guided-tours/visit-to-the-roofs/)). "401 steps" and "more than forty metres" (secondary, unverified).

**Model it**
- Giralda: a square shaft whose faces carry a central column of windows flanked by lozenge panels. The sebka net is texture; do not model it. The Renaissance belfry is a stack of set-back square stages with open arches, crowned by the Giraldillo.
- Cathedral mass: box nave and aisles with a forest of instanced pinnacles on the buttresses; flying buttresses are flat strips with alpha-cut arches.
- Patio: a grid of orange trees (billboards or low-poly "lollipops") in brick-edged beds.

**Play it**
- The Giralda is one continuous spiral ramp with no stairs, so mounted messengers or carts can use it. The seven stacked chambers are rooms at each level. The bells are the alarm objective.
- Roofs: gutters are walkways, buttress turrets hold stairs, flying buttresses bridge the roof levels and pinnacles give cover.
- The orange-tree court is dense cover with a fountain for noise, and the Puerta del Perdón is its gate.

**Images:** [Giralda](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sevilla_Cathedral_-_Giralda.jpg) · [Cathedral from the south-east](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sevilla_Cathedral_-_Southeast.jpg) · [Patio de los Naranjos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SevillaPatioDeLosNaranjos.jpg)

### 5.2 Burgos Cathedral (openwork spires and a lantern crossing)
**What and why:** the finest Spanish High Gothic silhouette: twin openwork spires and a lantern tower over the crossing.

**Dimensions**
- Outside 124 x 99 m, about 1.03 ha; the central nave is 11 m wide and 25 m high ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catedral_de_Burgos)).
- The west spires are 88 m; the crossing tower (*cimborrio*) is 59 m outside and 43 m inside ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catedral_de_Burgos)).
- Limestone from Hontoria de la Cantera; begun 1221; the spires date from 1442-1458 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgos_Cathedral)).

**Model it:** openwork spires are 8-sided cones carrying an alpha-cut tracery texture: pure silhouette. The crossing tower is an octagonal lantern ringed by pinnacles.

**Play it:** stair turrets lead to the spire bases. The lantern's inner gallery overlooks the crossing.

**Images:** [Burgos Cathedral](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Escaleras_a_la_Catedral_--_2023_--_Burgos,_Castilla_y_León,_España.jpg) · [Category: Cathedral of Burgos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Cathedral_of_Burgos) · [Category: Constable's Chapel](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Capilla_del_Condestable_%28Catedral_de_Burgos%29)

### 5.3 Batalha Monastery (Gothic and Manueline; the roofless chapels)
**What and why:** Portugal's great Gothic abbey, with a Manueline chapel left roofless and unfinished.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalha_Monastery))**
- "The church is vast and narrow (22 m) in proportion to its height (32.4 m)."
- The chapter house has a star vault over "a square space of 19 meters on the side" with no central support.
- The portal of the Unfinished Chapels "rises to a monumental fifteen metres".
- Founder's Chapel octagonal (1426-1434); Royal Cloister 1448-1477; built 1386 to about 1517 by fifteen architects.

**Model it:** the Unfinished Chapels are an octagon of massive buttress-piers with no roof: a gold silhouette under moonlight. The Manueline portal is one carved slab (texture).

**Play it:** the roofless chapel is an open-air arena with climbable pier tops. The unsupported 19 m vault is a room with nothing to hide behind.

**Images:** [Batalha](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Batalha_September_2021-2.jpg) · [Unfinished Chapels](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Batalha_September_2021-6.jpg) · [Category: Monastery of Batalha](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Monastery_of_Batalha)

### 5.4 Jerónimos Monastery and the Convent of Christ, Tomar (Manueline)
**What and why:** the two masterpieces of Manueline, Portugal's nautical late Gothic: a monastery with a vast two-storey cloister, and the most extravagant carved window in Iberia.

**Dimensions**
- Jerónimos south portal: 32 m high, 12 m wide ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerónimos_Monastery)).
- A single-span rib vault covers the 19 m wide church, on "six 25-metre-high, slender, articulated, octagonal columns". The transept vault (1522) "lacks any piers or columns" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerónimos_Monastery)).
- Two-storey cloister, 55 x 55 m; lioz limestone; begun 1501 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerónimos_Monastery)). An exterior of about 300 m (secondary, unverified).
- Tomar, the Charola (the Templar round church): a 16-sided exterior around an octagon inside ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent_of_Christ_(Tomar))).
- Tomar, the chapter-house window: the Cross of the Order of Christ, the armillary sphere, "ropes, corals and vegetal motifs", and a human figure at the bottom, probably the designer Diogo de Arruda. The convent has eight cloisters ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent_of_Christ_(Tomar))).

**Model it:** Manueline ornament lives in hero frames on otherwise plain walls: one portal slab and one window slab with alpha-cut rope and coral borders; everything else is plain cream stone. The palm-tree columns are cheap if the vault ribs are texture.

**Play it:** the two-storey cloister is the classic stealth loop, with the upper gallery overlooking the garden. The portal's niches make a climbable hero facade.

**Images:** [Jerónimos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Jerónimos_Monastery_or_Hieronymites_Monastery.png) · [Jerónimos cloister](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cloister_of_the_Jerónimos_Monastery_in_Belém,_Lisbon,_20250604_1314_9207.jpg) · [Tomar Manueline window](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Janela_Manuelina,_Convento_de_Cristo,_Tomar_2017-08-17.png)

### 5.5 Palma Cathedral, "La Seu" (the cathedral above the harbour)
**What and why:** a Gothic cathedral with one of the tallest naves in Europe, standing on the old sea wall: the cathedral-above-the-harbour silhouette.

**Dimensions**
- Size (conflict): 121 x 55 m, with an alternative 109 x 40 m, in [es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catedral_de_Santa_María_de_Palma_de_Mallorca); [en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_Cathedral) gives 121 x 40 m.
- Central vault 44 m (en.wiki) or 45 m (es.wiki); bell tower 47.8 m (es.wiki).
- The rose window is about 13.8 m across ("nearly 14 m") and almost 150 m² ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catedral_de_Santa_María_de_Palma_de_Mallorca)).
- A "double arcade" of flying buttresses; the pillars are "1/12th the width of the vault" (en.wiki); it overlooks the bay from the former Roman and Renaissance walls (es.wiki).

**Model it:** the double comb of flying buttresses along the sea front is the silhouette. Build one buttress module and array it.

**Play it:** the buttress comb is a ladder of ledges between the sea wall and the roof.

**Images:** [Palma Cathedral from the shore](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kathedrale_von_Palma_II.jpg)

### 5.6 Salamanca (golden stone; a cathedral you can walk inside the walls of)
**What and why:** a golden-sandstone cathedral whose towers, galleries and attic form a real walkable route, from the jail to the bells.

**Dimensions and features**
- New Cathedral: bell tower "93 meters" and also "92 meters", both in [en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Cathedral_of_Salamanca). Built of "freestone from Villamayor", with "two tribunes running around the perimeter of the cathedral and at two heights".
- Built 1512/1533-1733; its south wall rests on the Old Cathedral's north wall (en.wiki).
- The Ieronimus route, entered by the Torre Mocha, runs from the **Dungeon Room**, **Gaoler's Room** and Governor's Room ([ieronimus.es, official](https://ieronimus.es/en/)).
- It continues through the flat-topped tower, the Vault Room, a platform toward the Torre del Gallo, the **inner triforium of the New Cathedral**, a spiral stair, the Plaza de Anaya balcony, the **Attic of the New Cathedral**, the Clock Room and the Bells ([ieronimus.es, official](https://ieronimus.es/en/)). Part of the towers served as an ecclesiastical jail (secondary).

**Play it:** a real "cathedral as dungeon" route, readymade: jail in the tower base, then the triforium, the attic, the roof terraces and the bells.

**Images:** [New Cathedral of Salamanca](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WLM14ES_-_Catedral_Nueva_de_Salamanca._-_julianrdc_edited.jpg) · [Ieronimus, official](https://ieronimus.es/en/)

### 5.7 Supporting: Toledo and León
- **Toledo:** 120 m long, 59 m wide, 44.5 m high; five naves; tower 92 m; white limestone from Olihuelas; the Transparente, 1729-1732 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_Cathedral)). [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:FP_Toledo_Cathedral_2025_-_West_façade.jpg)
- **León:** 90 m long, 29 m wide, 30 m high; "125 windows, with 1800 meters² of polychrome stained glass"; walls reduced "to their minimum expression" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/León_Cathedral)). [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Catedral_Gótica_de_León.jpg)
- **Crypt:** I found no strong, dimensioned Iberian cathedral-crypt reference. For the crypt kit, use Coimbra's cryptoporticus (7.4) or the Cáceres cistern (7.1); Salamanca's tower jail (5.6) is the alternative "below the cathedral" space.

## 6. The gorge and the great bridge

### 6.1 Ronda: Puente Nuevo, El Tajo, the water mine and the mills (BEST)
**What and why:** a town split by a deep gorge and joined by a masonry bridge with a prison in its arch, with mills, baths and a secret stair to the river below. The brief's gorge district almost exactly.

**The bridge**
- Height 98 m ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_Nuevo_(Ronda)); [Discover Ronda](https://www.discoverronda.com/puente-nuevo.html)).
- Length (conflict): 66 m ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_Nuevo)) or 70 m (es.wiki). Three spans (en.wiki).
- Built 1759-1793, 34 years (en.wiki; es.wiki also gives 1751 as the start). The first bridge, begun 1735, collapsed in 1741, killing 50 people. Ashlar from Arroyo del Toro (es.wiki).
- "There is a chamber above the central arch that was used for a variety of purposes, including as a prison". It is "entered through a square building that was once the guardhouse" and now holds an exhibition (en.wiki).
- Both sides in the 1936-39 civil war allegedly used the chamber as a torture room and threw prisoners from its windows (en.wiki, via search summary).

**The gorge:** El Tajo is "100-plus-meter-deep" ([en.wiki Ronda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronda)); the Puente Nuevo article calls it a "120-metre-deep chasm".

**The mills** ([desfiladerodeltajo.info](https://desfiladerodeltajo.info/10-las-acequias-arabes-y-molinos-harineros-del-tajo/))
- "Up to thirteen flour mills and fulling mills" stood on the slope, most with two millstones.
- Their channels were cut into the rock slope with "siphons, chutes, vaults, overflow channels, and tunnels". Two mill buildings survive.
- **"A small passage was built into the Puente Nuevo's right pier to allow the acequia to continue functioning."**
- The Camino de los Molinos followed the inner line of the Muslim wall (secondary).

**The water mine under the Casa del Rey Moro** (all secondary: [Ronda Today](https://rondatoday.com/rondas-water-mine-under-the-casa-del-rey-moro/))
- A 14th-c. stair cut inside the cliff down to the river, for water during sieges: 231 steps down about 60 m.
- Rooms on the way include a domed "Hall of Secrets" and a weapons room. Enslaved workers passed full leather water-skins up the stair hand to hand.
- Gardens by J. C. N. Forestier.

**Model it**
- The bridge is a massive masonry wall pierced by stacked arches, wedged deep in the V of the gorge; the chamber's windows show above the central arch.
- Gorge walls are big rock meshes with a vertex-colour gradient: lighter tops, dark wet bottoms. Fill the gorge with fog, which gives vertical depth for free and hides the floor.

**Play it**
- The prison chamber in the arch is reached from the guardhouse (front door) or by climbing the pier.
- The mill channel's passage through the pier is a secret crawl through the bridge.
- The water mine is a hidden stair from a palace to the river, with fixed rooms on the way.
- The mills are noisy machinery (sound cover) strung along a cliff path.

**Images:** [Puente Nuevo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Punte_Nuevo_Bridge,_Ronda_-_Spain.jpg) · [Ronda aerial](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ronda_aerial.jpg) · [Casa del Rey Moro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ronda-Casa_del_Rey_Moro-20110912.jpg)

The Casa del Rey Moro photo's Commons description says "the tunnel on the platform is the outlet" of the mine. More: [Bridge seen from the Camino de los Molinos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mirador_del_Puente_Nuevo_de_Ronda,_Spain.jpg) · [Category: Puente Nuevo, Ronda](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Puente_Nuevo,_Ronda)

### 6.2 Cuenca: the hanging houses and the San Pablo bridge
**What and why:** medieval houses with wooden balconies cantilevered over a gorge, facing a tall footbridge.

**Dimensions**
- Three buildings survive: the Casa de la Sirena and the two Casas de los Reyes, built 13th-15th c. ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casas_Colgadas)).
- They have cantilevered parts "or large balconies" over the Huécar gorge; first drawn in 1565 by Anton van den Wyngaerde ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casas_Colgadas)). The best-known group is three houses with wooden balconies ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_Houses_of_Cuenca)).
- San Pablo bridge: an iron-and-wood footbridge of 1902-1903, "106 m long in two spans" and "39 m maximum height" ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_de_San_Pablo_(Cuenca))).
- It rests on the stone piers of the earlier stone bridge (1533-1589), which collapsed; the article gives no date ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_de_San_Pablo_(Cuenca))).

**Model it:** a masonry base on the rock, with timber-framed upper floors and continuous wooden balconies on projecting beams. The beams must be geometry because they are the silhouette; the rest is texture.

**Play it:** balcony runs along the cliff are the most exposed traversal in the level; the beams underneath are hang-and-shimmy lines.

**Images:** [Casas Colgadas](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casas_Colgadas.jpg) · [San Pablo bridge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Puente_de_San_Pablo_%28Cuenca%29.jpg) · [1879 engraving of the bridge and gorge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1879,_Picturesque_Europe,_vol_III,_Puente_San_Pablo,_Cuenca.jpg)

### 6.3 Setenil de las Bodegas (houses under the rock)
**What and why:** a town whose streets run beneath overhanging rock.

**Features**
- Dwellings built into a rock formation above the Guadalporcún (Trejo) ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setenil_de_las_Bodegas)).
- The house type is *abrigo bajo roca*: rock shelters closed off with a facade rather than dug out. Cave streets: Cuevas del Sol, Cuevas de la Sombra, Herrería, Jabonería, Cabreriza, Triana, Cuevas de San Román ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setenil_de_las_Bodegas)).
- A Nasrid castle with a keep; 530 m of walls with about 40 towers ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setenil_de_las_Bodegas), as summarised; unverified).

**Model it:** a long rock-overhang mesh forms the street's ceiling, with house facades as planes beneath it. Strong vertex-colour shadow.

**Play it:** streets roofed by rock are dark by default. The top of the overhang is a walkable field above the town.

**Images:** [Setenil](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Setenil_de_las_Bodegas_-_001_%2830708392785%29.jpg) · [Calle Cuevas del Sol](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Calle-cueva-del-sol.jpg) · [Category: Setenil](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Setenil_de_las_Bodegas)

### 6.4 Castellfollit de la Roca (houses on the cliff edge)
**What and why:** a village strung along the lip of a sheer basalt cliff.

**Features:** a basalt cliff "over 50 metres high and almost a kilometre long", formed by two overlapping lava flows, where the Fluvià and the Toronell meet ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellfollit_de_la_Roca)).

**Model it:** houses flush with a vertical basalt face; the column-jointed basalt is texture on a faceted cliff mesh.

**Images:** [Castellfollit](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castellfollit_de_la_Roca_des_del_campanar_vell_-_panoramio.jpg) · [Category: Castellfollit de la Roca](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Castellfollit_de_la_Roca)

### 6.5 Fortified bridges: Toledo's San Martín and the Roman bridge at Alcántara
**What and why:** medieval and Roman bridges defended by gate towers and a mid-span arch: the checkpoint versions of the great bridge.

**Dimensions**
- San Martín: "five arches, with the largest in the middle having a span of 40 meters"; late 14th c. (Archbishop Pedro Tenorio); towers at the ends, the newer from the 16th c.; a legend of the architect's wife ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_de_San_Martín_(Toledo))).
- Alcántara (Roman, Extremadura): 181.7 m long, 8.6 m wide, 45 m from river bed to deck, excluding the triumphal arch at mid-span ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcántara_Bridge)).
- Its six spans are 13.6, 23.4, 28.8, 27.4, 21.9 and 13.8 m; built 104-106 AD ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcántara_Bridge)).

**Play it:** the gate towers are checkpoints at both ends. A mid-span arch is a gate in the middle of the bridge, which splits it into two lockable halves.

**Images:** [San Martín bridge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Puente_San_Martin_Toledo.jpg) · [Alcántara bridge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_puente_de_Alcántara,_Cáceres.jpg)

### 6.6 Caminito del Rey (the cliff path)
**What and why:** a narrow walkway pinned to a gorge wall high above the river: the cliff-path reference.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caminito_del_Rey))**
- "1 metre in width", rising "over 100 metres above the river".
- About 3 km (2.9 km after renovation); built 1901-1905; the original ran on "steel rails supported by stanchions built at approximately 45 degrees".

**Model it and play it:** a thin plank strip on angled brackets pinned to the gorge wall. Cheap to build and terrifying to cross; a broken section makes a hang-and-shimmy gap.

**Images:** [Caminito del Rey](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Caminito_del_Rey_3.jpg) · [Category: Caminito del Rey](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Caminito_del_Rey)

### 6.7 Albolafia, Córdoba (the river waterwheel)
**What and why:** a medieval waterwheel beside Córdoba's Roman bridge that lifted river water up to the palace.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albolafia))**
- A wheel "15 meters" in diameter turned by the current, raising water "into an aqueduct that delivered water to the city and to the nearby Alcazar".
- Three horseshoe arches of that aqueduct survived into the early 20th c.
- The wheel was dismantled in 1492 on Isabella's orders because its noise bothered her.

**Play it:** a constant noise field and a moving object you can ride up. Stop the wheel and the whole gorge goes quiet.

**Images:** [Albolafia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Córdoba_2015_10_23_2593_%2825944141760%29_%28cropped%29.jpg)

## 7. The undercroft

### 7.1 Cistern of the Casa de las Veletas, Cáceres (BEST: columns standing in water)
**What and why:** a Moorish cistern under a palace, with horseshoe arches on columns standing in still water.

**Dimensions**
- Rectangular, "13.50 x 9.90 m and 137 m²". It is roofed by "five brick barrel vaults" resting on "sixteen horseshoe arches and twelve monolithic columns". Rainwater enters "through the floor paving" of the courtyard above ([Hispania Nostra Red List](https://listaroja.hispanianostra.org/ficha/aljibe-hispano-musulman-del-palacio-de-las-veletas/)).
- [es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_las_Veletas) describes an irregular plan partly cut into the rock, "attempting a rectangle of 15 x 10 m", with four parallel arcades of four brick horseshoe arches each.
- The columns are reused, including a Roman votive altar and possibly Visigothic capitals; it is dated to either the Caliphal or the Almohad period ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_las_Veletas)). Nave height 5.30 m (secondary, unverified).

**Model it:** a room of only about 140 m². Sixteen arches, twelve columns and five vaults are few polygons; the water plane and the vertex-coloured reflections of the arches are the whole look.

**Play it:** wading is noisy; the arch springings are a balance route. Grates in the courtyard above are the entry. Shallow water puts out torches and hides dropped items.

**Images:** [The cistern](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aljibe_almohade,_Cáceres_%2815997099967%29.jpg)

### 7.2 The Initiation Well, Quinta da Regaleira (Sintra)
**What and why:** a 20th-c. esoteric garden folly: a spiral well, tunnels and grottoes built as an initiation route.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinta_da_Regaleira))**
- A "27-metre 'inverted tower' featuring a monumental spiral staircase" in nine flights, with "23 small niches". At the bottom, "an inland stone compass with the Templar cross".
- The smaller Unfinished Well has "straight stairs that connect a series of ring-shaped floors".
- Tunnels lead to grottoes, to the chapel crypt ("which connects directly to the main palace via an underground walkway") and to the Waterfall Lake.
- A 4 ha estate; built 1904-1910 by Luigi Manini.

**Model it:** a cylinder shaft with a helical ledge and an arcade (a column every N degrees), open to the sky at the top. The moss vertex colour darkens with depth.

**Play it:** guards at the rim can see down the shaft, partly blocked by the arcade. The tunnel hub links the undercroft to the palace gardens, with a lake exit across stepping stones.

**Images:** [View down the well](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:View_down_the_Well.jpg) · [Well entrance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sintra_-_Quinta_da_Regaleira_-_Entrada_do_Poço_iniciático.jpg) · [Fisheye of the well](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fisheye-hemi-hemi_initiation_well.jpg)

### 7.3 The Chapel of Bones, Évora (the ossuary chapel)
**What and why:** a Franciscan chapel lined with human bones: the ossuary.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capela_dos_Ossos))**
- "Three spans 18.7 m long and 11 m wide".
- "Its walls and eight pillars are decorated in carefully arranged bones and skulls held together by cement", from an estimated 5,000 exhumed corpses.
- "The ceiling is made of white painted brick and is painted with death motifs", with the phrase *Melior est dies mortis die nativitatis*.
- "Light enters through three small openings on the left". Two desiccated corpses, one a child, lie in glass cases.
- The entrance inscription reads "Nós ossos que aqui estamos pelos vossos esperamos": "We bones that are here await yours" (en.wiki gives the translation; the Portuguese is from secondary sources).

**Model it:** bones are a tiling texture (rows of skulls, bands of femurs) on plain walls and pillars. Texture does all the work; the geometry is a simple three-bay vault.

**Play it:** a shrine room whose only light is three small windows, so it is naturally dark.

**Images:** [Capela dos Ossos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Capilla_de_los_huesos,_Évora.jpg) · [Category: Capela dos Ossos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Capela_dos_Ossos)

### 7.4 Cryptoporticus of Aeminium, Coimbra (a real two-level Roman maze with published measurements)
**What and why:** the two-storey Roman substructure of a forum: a maze of galleries with every passage measured.

**Features and dimensions** ([Persée, 1973](https://www.persee.fr/doc/efr_0000-0000_1973_act_14_1_1604?pageId=T1_349); [Alarcão et al. 2017](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/24b3/e5db891c52ca2eea73941c44ef0b64b84a93.pdf))
- A two-storey podium of vaulted galleries that makes a level platform for the forum on a steep slope. It was built under Claudius and Nero, reusing an Augustan cryptoporticus (Alarcão).
- The tallest (western) facade rose about 29 m, with a fountain at its base; the museum, a former bishop's palace, stands on top (Alarcão).
- **Upper floor:** two Π-shaped galleries, one inside the other, linked by three passages in each wing. Between the arms, seven small cells are linked by narrow passages in line with the two entrances, so from one entrance you see the other through all seven cells (Persée).
- Upper floor, maximum internal size: 32.65 m (east-west) x 45.70 m (north-south).
- Galleries are 2.95 m wide (one is 3.5 m) under 4.40 m vaults.
- Cells are 2.90 x 4.30 m (outer ones 2.60 x 4.30 m, the central one 2.60 x 3.70 m) under 3.25 m vaults.
- Passages between cells: 0.75 m wide, 1.15 m long, vaults 1.80 m high. Cell floors are 0.90 m below the gallery floors.
- Entrances are 1.30 m wide and 2.10 m high; walls are 1.70-2.20 m thick; air and light come through slit windows (Persée).
- **Lower floor:** a barrel-vaulted gallery 60 m long and 2.95 m wide, with two exedrae and seven larger rooms opening off it, and stairs at both ends (Persée).

**Model it and play it:** use these dimensions literally. The 0.75 x 1.80 m cell passages are "squeeze" traversal. The line of seven cells is a sightline trap: a guard at one door sees through all of them.

**Images:** [Cryptoporticus gallery](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cryptoporticus_of_Aeminium_-_Museu_Nacional_de_Machado_de_Castro_-_Coimbra,_Portugal_-_DSC09856.jpg) · [Cryptoporticus](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ForumRomanoDeCoimbra1.jpg)

### 7.5 Roman galleries of the Rua da Prata, Lisbon (flooded tunnels under the street)
**What and why:** Roman tunnels under the old town that stay flooded and open only when pumped out: the smugglers' tunnels.

**Features ([pt.wiki](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galerias_Romanas_da_Rua_da_Prata))**
- "Vaulted corridors, parallel to each other, approximately 3 metres high and 2 to 3 metres wide", with round-arched vaults.
- Flooded by groundwater. Firefighters pump them dry twice a year (usually April and September) so the public can visit.
- The entrance is "a manhole cover in the middle of the street" in Rua da Conceição.
- Found in 1770-1771 during rebuilding after the earthquake; a cryptoporticus, once called *conservas de água* (water tanks).

**Play it:** a smugglers' route that only opens when drained, which ties into the Register House valves (3.1).

**Images:** [Galerias Romanas](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Galerias_Romanas.jpg)

### 7.6 Cuevas de Hércules, Toledo (a cistern under a vanished church)
**What and why:** a Roman cistern under Toledo wrapped in legends of necromancy.

**Dimensions and features ([Consorcio de Toledo, official](https://consorciotoledo.com/cuevas-de-hercules/))**
- A Roman water tank, 2nd half of the 1st c. AD, "6.00 m wide by at least 11.50 m long and 4.00 high".
- Built of small stones in lime-and-sand mortar and lined with hydraulic cement.
- Later lined with granite ashlars and split lengthwise by "three large arches equally of granite" into two naves vaulted with white limestone blocks.
- A church of San Ginés stood above from about the 12th c. Legends make the caves Hercules' palace of "magical arts and necromancy", and the site of the sealed chest whose opening brought "the destruction of the kingdom".

**Play it:** a lore room built around the sealed chest; with the church above gone, the entrance is from a courtyard.

**Images:** [Entrance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Caves_of_Hercules,_Toledo,_view_of_the_entry.JPG) · [Interior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cueva_de_Hércules_-_01.jpg)

### 7.7 Roman necropolis of Carmona (the Iberian "catacomb")
**What and why:** a Roman cemetery of rock-cut family chambers, the closest Iberian match to catacombs.

**Features ([Carmona tourist office, official](https://turismo.carmona.org/en/necropolis/))**
- The family chamber tomb "is reached through a shallow shaft cut into the rock, with very basic stairs". The chamber is usually square, "with a bench that runs around the lower part of the walls" and niches for urns.
- Tomb of Servilia: "the largest", built like "a luxurious mansion, with a large porticoed courtyard", with a trapezoidal vestibule "covered by a pointed vault".
- Tomb of the Elephant: "a sanctuary dedicated to the worship of the gods Cybele and Attis" (official). It is cut into the rock, with funeral banquet benches carved from it (secondary, via search summary).
- Hundreds of chambers, used from the 1st c. BC to the 4th c. AD (secondary).

**Model it:** three kit pieces (shaft, chamber, niche wall), dressed with urns.

**Images:** [Tomb of Servilia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carmona-Necrópolis_Romana-Tumba_de_Serviliae-20110916.jpg) · [Tomb of the Elephant](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carmona_necrópolis_elefante82.JPG)

### 7.8 The Alhambra's silo dungeons (the oubliette)
**What and why:** rock-cut grain silos used as prisons inside the Alhambra's fortress: the oubliette.

**Dimensions ([Patronato de la Alhambra, official](https://www.alhambra-patronato.es/las-mazmorras-de-la-alhambra))**
- Grain silos reused as prisons: "an inverted funnel shape, circular floor plan, and small openings".
- About 5-7 m deep with a base 6-8 m across (after Torres Balbás).
- Prisoners were lowered and raised with ropes or ladders. Münzer counted "at least fourteen" in 1494, in the Alcazaba, the Plaza de Armas and elsewhere.

**Play it:** a pit you can drop into but cannot climb out of without a rope: a puzzle, a trap and a drop-kill spot.

## 8. The castle on the summit (Iberian alcazar elements)

### 8.1 Alcázar of Segovia (BEST SILHOUETTE)
**What and why:** a royal castle on a prow of rock between two rivers, with slate spires and a deep moat: the most famous Iberian castle silhouette.

**Dimensions and features**
- A castle on a rock crag where the Eresma and Clamores rivers meet ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcázar_of_Segovia)).
- "To reach the castle one must cross a defensive moat 26 metres deep"; hewn blocks are still visible at 23 m ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcázar_de_Segovia)).
- Tower of John II: "80 metres" high per es.wiki (unverified; likely measured from the valley rather than the tower alone). Steps (conflict): 152 on a spiral stair (es.wiki) versus "two steep and spiral staircases with 156 steps" (en.wiki).
- Slate roofs were added under Philip II in the 16th c. (es.wiki). First mentioned 1120; fire of 1862. Its silhouette inspired the castle in Disney's *Snow White* (1937) (en.wiki).

**Model it:** a ship-prow plan tapering to a point over the cliff. The keep has a ring of round bartizans (*garitones*) around its top, and the slate spires are dark blue-grey cones: these are the recognisable parts. Walls carry the Segovian sgraffito pattern as texture (see 9.8).

**Play it:** the moat and drawbridge are the only front door. The rock prow is the climbing route. The bartizans are overwatch nests.

**Images:** [Alcázar of Segovia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Panorámica_Otoño_Alcázar_de_Segovia.jpg) · [Category: Alcázar of Segovia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Alcázar_of_Segovia)

### 8.2 Alcazaba of Málaga and Gibralfaro (BEST LAYOUT)
**What and why:** a Moorish citadel of nested walls and a chain of gates, joined by a walled corridor to a hilltop castle: the layered-summit reference.

**Dimensions and features**
- "Two walled enclosures, one contained within the other" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba_of_Málaga)).
- "Five gates had to be crossed in order to reach the palace areas": the Puerta de la Bóveda, de las Columnas, the Arco de Cristo, Cuartos de Granada and de los Arcos.
- Inner patios: of the Oranges, of the Pool, and of the Cistern (about 25 m²).
- The Airón well "cuts into the bedrock to a depth of 30 meters". Built from the 11th c., modified to the 14th c.; besieged in 1487 (en.wiki).
- Gibralfaro stands on a 130 m hill. A double-walled corridor (*coracha*) links it to the Alcazaba; Yusuf I extended it in the early 14th c. ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro_Castle)).

**Model it and play it:** nested enclosures and a chain of gates make layered checkpoints. The coracha is a walled corridor climbing the slope between two fortresses: a guarded tube, or a run along its wall-top.

**Images:** [Alcazaba overview](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alcazaba_de_Málaga_overview.jpg) · [Gibralfaro hill and castle](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Viviendas_de_El_Ejido.jpg) · [Category: Alcazaba of Málaga](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Alcazaba_of_Málaga)

### 8.3 Castelo de São Jorge, Lisbon (the city's crown)
**What and why:** Lisbon's Moorish castle crowning the Alfama: the city-crown reference.

**Features**
- The city wall was repaired in 985 under Almanzor; the castle was conquered in 1147 ([official](https://castelodesaojorge.pt/en/castle/national-monument/the-walls/)).
- Gates: the Porta de São Jorge, the Porta do Espírito Santo (with an armillary sphere) and the Porta de Martim Moniz (official).
- The castle is rectangular "with ten towers" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Jorge_Castle)); eleven (secondary).
- The later Fernandine city wall (1373) had "46 gateways and 77 towers" (official); en.wiki gives "34 or 38 gates" and a 5,400 m perimeter (conflict).
- A camera obscura in the Tower of Ulysses since 1998 (en.wiki). An archaeological site with Moorish houses and the ruins of the palace destroyed in 1755 (secondary).

**Play it:** the camera obscura is an in-world scouting device that shows the city below: a map room. Ten towers make a ring of overwatch.

**Images:** [São Jorge Castle](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:LisbonCastle.jpg) · [Category: Castelo de São Jorge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Castelo_de_São_Jorge)

### 8.4 Castelo dos Mouros, Sintra (walls along a granite ridge)
**What and why:** Moorish walls snaking along a granite mountain ridge.

**Dimensions ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_of_the_Moors))**
- A "450-metre perimeter on top of a mountainous cliff", with "five rectangular towers and one circular tower, surmounted by pyramidal merlons". The highest, the "royal tower", stands at the south-west corner.
- A cistern "18 metres long by 6 metres wide, and 6 metres in height", reached by a 3 m passage.
- Built in the 8th-9th c. and taken in 1147; conserved from 1840 by Ferdinand II; about 471 m above sea level.

**Model it:** pyramidal merlons are the Moorish crenellation. Use them to tell the Moorish walls apart from the English-style castle's square merlons.

**Play it:** the ridge-top wall-walk is a traversal ribbon along the summit, with boulders as cover.

**Images:** [Castelo dos Mouros panorama](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castelo_dos_Mouros,_Sintra,_Portugal,_2019-05-25,_DD_112-121_PAN.jpg) · [Category: Castelo dos Mouros](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Castelo_dos_Mouros)

### 8.5 Peñafiel Castle (the ship on the ridge)
**What and why:** a long, thin castle that runs along a ridge like a ship.

**Dimensions:** "more than 200 metres long", enclosing "an area about 35 m wide"; "a 34 m high three storey keep" stands in the middle ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peñafiel_Castle)).

**Model it:** a long, thin hull of walls with one tall keep amidships. It reads from the far side of the gorge.

**Images:** [Peñafiel](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castello_di_peñafiel.jpg) · [Category: Castle of Peñafiel](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Castle_of_Peñafiel)

### 8.6 Almourol (the island castle)
**What and why:** a Templar castle on an islet in the Tagus.

**Dimensions:** an islet about 310 x 75 m in the Tagus, on a granite outcrop 18 m high; walls with "nine tall circular towers"; a three-storey keep; rebuilt from 1171 (an inscription over the gate) under the Templar master Gualdim Pais ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almourol_Castle)).

**Images:** [Almourol](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Almourol_034.jpg)

### 8.7 Coca Castle (brick Mudéjar)
**What and why:** a 15th-c. brick castle where the brick is also the ornament.

**Features ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castillo_de_Coca))**
- A square lower enclosure with four corner towers, the keep the largest, surrounded by "a wide and deep moat".
- Mostly 15th-c. brick, "used not only as construction material but also as a decorative element"; limestone for arrow slits and courtyard columns.
- Licensed in 1453 and practically complete by 1493; interiors with stucco and painted decoration.

**Model it:** brick pattern bands are texture; the polygonal turrets are geometry.

**Images:** [Coca Castle](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castele_01.JPG) · [Category: Castle of Coca](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Castle_of_Coca)

### 8.8 Supporting: the Alcazaba of Almería and albarrana towers
- **Almería:** three enclosures; the Muralla de Jayrán wall climbs the neighbouring hill ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba_of_Almería); Commons images). [Alcazaba](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alcazaba_de_Almería.jpg) · [Muralla de Jayrán](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Almeria_Muralla_de_Jayran_%28fcm%29.jpg)
- **Albarrana tower:** "a defensive tower detached from the curtain wall and connected to it by a bridge or an arcade". Removable wooden sections could isolate a captured tower ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albarrana_tower)).
- Albarrana towers peaked in the Almohad period. Examples: the Torre de Espantaperros (Badajoz, 1170), the Torre del Oro and Torre de la Plata (Seville) and the Torre de la Malmuerta (Córdoba). Early ones are pentagonal or octagonal ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albarrana_tower)). [Torre de la Malmuerta, whose arch spans a street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cordoue_-_Tour_de_la_Malmuerta_2.JPG)

### Alcazar element checklist (drawn from 8.1-8.8 and 7.8)
- Keep (*torre del homenaje*); nested enclosures reached through a chain of gates; a walled corridor (*coracha*) to water or to a second fort.
- Albarrana towers on arches, with removable bridges; pyramidal merlons (Moorish) against square merlons (the English-style parts).
- Round bartizans and slate cones (Segovia); a dry moat and drawbridge.
- Cisterns under the parade ground and wells cut into bedrock (the Pozo Airón, 30 m); silo dungeons.

## 9. Cross-cutting material notes

### 9.1 Whitewash and lime
- The white towns are "characterised by whitewashed walls and red or brown tiled roofs". Whitewash was introduced in Roman times but "became predominant... during the pandemic plague waves during 14th and later centuries" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Towns_of_Andalusia)).
- **PS2:** a whitewash texture is never flat white. Use a 256 px tile with brush streaks, grey patches where coats have flaked, and ochre-grey soiling at the base; add soiling and rising damp in vertex colour along the bottom 0.5-1 m (my recommendation). Under a moonlit night grade, whitewash is the brightest surface in the city, so it drives stealth readability. Keep the old town's alleys narrow so whitewash stays in shadow except where lamps hit it.

### 9.2 Azulejo tiles (blue-and-white and polychrome)
- **Spain** ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azulejo)):
  - *Alicatado* (13th c.): cut-tile mosaic assembled into geometric patterns.
  - *Cuenca/arista* (late 15th and early 16th c.): Seville became the production centre, using moulds that leave "thin ridges of clay" between the colours. (*Cuerda seca*, the dry-line technique, is also Spanish; its dates were not verified.)
  - The Casa de Pilatos (1530s) "has around 150 different azulejo designs".
- **Portugal** ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azulejo)):
  - King Manuel I brought Seville tiles back after a visit in 1503, and Portugal adopted *horror vacui*, covering whole walls.
  - 17th-c. polychrome "carpet" patterns (*azulejo de tapete*); from the 1680s, blue-and-white figurative panels under Delft influence.
  - Placement: church dados, cloisters, palaces, garden fountains and altar fronts. In the 19th c., "the Portuguese took over the Brazilian fashion of decorating the façades of their houses with azulejos".
- The Alhambra wall scheme is tiles on lower walls and floors, stucco above ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra)).
- **Where to put them in our city** (my recommendation):
  - Old town: full or partial tiled house facades, Portuguese style.
  - Churches, cloisters and palaces: tiled dados roughly to waist or shoulder height, with blue-and-white figurative panels in the cathedral cloister.
  - Moorish palace: geometric *alicatado* dados.
  - Miradouro benches and fountains: tiled panels.
- **PS2:** tile panels are pure texture. A tiled facade should still carry a vertex-colour gradient and grime, or it reads as wallpaper.
- **Images:** [Fully tiled facade (Porto)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Azulejo_facade_of_the_Capela_das_Almas_in_Porto_%284%29.jpg) · [Alicatado dado (Alhambra)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alicatados_de_la_Sala_del_Mexuar,_la_Alhambra.jpg) · [Santa Luzia panels](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lisboa,_Miradouro_de_Santa_Luzia,_azulejos_%281%29.jpg)

### 9.3 Terracotta roofs and floors
- **Roofs:** the *teja árabe* is a tapered, curved tile, usually about 45 cm long, 21 and 16 cm wide at its two ends, 8 cm high and about 2 kg ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teja_árabe)).
  - Laid about 27 per m² with at least 20% overlap, in alternating rows: *canal* (concave side up) and *cobija* (convex side up, covering the joints).
  - Despite the name it is Roman in origin (4th-5th c.) ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teja_árabe)).
  - **PS2:** a roof plane with a corrugated canal-and-cobija texture and a modelled eaves edge (a few scalloped tile ends) along the silhouette only. Portuguese houses often have layered tile eaves; the pt.wiki article (*beiral*) only defines the eaves as "the last row of tiles" ([pt.wiki](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiral)), so the multi-row variants are (unverified).
- **Floors:** the *olambrilla* is "a square tile of about seven centimetres per side" set among larger unglazed terracotta bricks, typical of Seville patios ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olambrilla); [image: Casa de Pilatos floor](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_de_Pilatos_26.jpg)).
- [Roofscape image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:San_Agustin_roofs_C.jpg)

### 9.4 Moorish stucco (*yesería*), arches, the *alfiz* and *sebka*
- **Horseshoe arch:** the Córdoban caliphal version is "about three quarters of a circle", more circular than the Visigothic one; pointed horseshoe arches appear from the Almoravids on ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_arch)). (Exact proportions: not found.)
- **Córdoba's two-tier arches:** "a lower tier of horseshoe arches and an upper tier of semi-circular arches". "The voussoirs of the arches alternate between red brick and white stone"; "approximately 850 columns made of jasper, onyx, marble, granite and porphyry". The mihrab has "interlacing multifoil arches" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque–Cathedral_of_Córdoba)). [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arcos_interiores_Mezquita_de_Córdoba_04.jpg)
- **Alfiz:** "a moulding, usually a rectangular panel, which encloses the outward side of an arch". The spandrels between arch and frame "are usually richly decorated" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfiz)).
- **Sebka:** a "net" of interlacing rhombus motifs, taken from Córdoba's interlaced arches. Almohad builders put it on minarets in brick (the Giralda); Nasrid builders carved it in stucco on walls. One variant interlaces polylobed arches ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebka)).
- **PS2:** arch *outlines* are geometry (the horseshoe curve must be in the silhouette, 12-16 segments). Voussoir striping, the alfiz panel, the stucco field and the sebka net are all textures. Interlaced polylobed screens are alpha cut-outs.

### 9.5 Muqarnas (*mocárabes*)
- "Rows or tiers of niche-like elements are projected over others below", used in domes, portals, squinches, capitals and cornices; in stucco or plaster in the western Islamic world; the Spanish term is *mocárabe* ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqarnas)). The Hall of the Two Sisters has at least 5,000 cells.
- **PS2:** model 3-5 stepped rings of faceted cells (or even concentric stepped rings with a cell texture); bake ambient occlusion per ring. Put them only in hero rooms and gate soffits.

### 9.6 Manueline carving
- The motifs: "columns carved like twisted strands of rope", the armillary sphere, anchors, seaweed and the cross of the Order of Christ ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manueline)).
- Plants: "laurel branches, oak leaves, acorns, poppy capsules, corncobs, and thistles". It prefers "semicircular arches (instead of Gothic pointed arches)"; about 1490-1520 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manueline)).
- Key works: Jerónimos, Belém, and Tomar's chapter-house window.
- **PS2:** rope mouldings are thin twisted cylinders (6 sides, spiral via texture) or just a band texture. Keep the carving in hero frames: portals, windows, the tops of turrets.

### 9.7 Iberian ironwork
- *Rejas* are "an architectural element of metal or wood used to close windows or other openings" ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reja_(arquitectura))). Types include fixed grilles and removable folding ones (*rejas de ballesta*).
- Spanish Gothic *rejería* of the 15th-17th c. screened chapels and choirs; Plateresque rejería turned the bars into slender embossed balusters ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reja_(arquitectura))).
- **PS2 and gameplay:** window grilles and balcony rails are alpha cards on a thin box. Projecting grilles are climbing holds and a way to peek into lit rooms. The cathedral's chapel and choir screens are lockable gates. Lanterns (not researched in detail) are simple 4-6-sided cages with an emissive core.
- **Images:** [Reja](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reja-dragones.jpg) · [Window grille, Córdoba](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22Pelando_la_pava%22.JPG)

### 9.8 Wall-surface systems (all textures)
- **Aparejo toledano:** brick courses framing rubble-stone panels with brick corners ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparejo_toledano)). [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Pueblanueva_%282023%29_11.jpg)
- **Segovian sgraffito (*esgrafiado*):** "templates made of wood, metal or cardboard" press geometric, symmetrical motifs into fresh mortar, then the top coat is scraped to show the layer beneath. Found in Turégano, Coca and Pedraza ([es.wiki](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esgrafiado)). This is the Alcázar of Segovia's wall finish. [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Esgrafiado-segoviano.jpg) · [Detail, Segovia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Detalle_del_esgrafiado_de_la_fachada_de_un_edificio_de_Segovia_01.jpg)
- ***Opus mixtum*** (Mérida): granite ashlar with bands of red brick ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acueducto_de_los_Milagros)).
- **Rammed earth, red** (Alhambra): see 4.1.
- **Mudéjar brick towers:** for example the 14th-c. Torre de El Salvador, Teruel (Commons description). [Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torre_de_El_Salvador._Teruel.jpg) Its glazed ceramic inserts are visible in photos; not text-verified.

### 9.9 Stone: granite against sandstone against limestone
- **Villamayor stone (Salamanca):** an arkosic sandstone of Middle Eocene age, 40-70% quartz and 10-30% feldspar in a clay matrix. It is weak but "malleable and ideal for detailed carving", and iron oxides give it an "ochreous/golden" patina, hence "the Golden City" (IUGS and Lyell abstracts, via search summary: [IUGS](https://iugs-geoheritage.org/geoheritage_stones/villamayor-stone-golden-stone/)). The New Cathedral is "freestone from Villamayor" ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Cathedral_of_Salamanca)).
- **Lioz (Lisbon):** a limestone whose "color is generally ivory but varies from light grey to whitish and rosy", with 120-million-year-old rudist fossils. It was called "royal stone" (*pedra real*) and was used for Jerónimos and Belém; a Global Heritage Stone Resource ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lioz)).
- **Granite:** Segovia's aqueduct (mortarless ashlar) and the Alcántara bridge are granite; Porto is "the granite city" (secondary).
- **Other limestones:** Toledo Cathedral is "white limestone from the quarries of Olihuelas"; Burgos is Hontoria limestone (en.wiki).
- **Brick:** the Giralda's main body and Seville's Atarazanas; Coca Castle.
- **PS2 suggestion:** give each district one dominant stone so it reads under fog. Grey granite for the harbour, bridges and aqueduct; whitewash and terracotta for the old town; red earth and pinkish brick for the palace and Moorish walls; golden sandstone for the cathedral; cream lioz for Manueline hero frames.

### 9.10 Pavements
- *Calçada portuguesa*, small black basalt and white limestone cubes in patterns, is **19th-century**: a zigzag at São Jorge castle (1840-1846) and the Rossio wave pattern of 1848 ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_pavement)). Use it only if the setting tolerates that era. Andalusian pebble paving and brick-and-*olambrilla* floors (9.3) are the older options.

## 10. Modelling rules digest

1. **Plain outside, rich inside; ornament only in hero frames.** "Buildings were designed to be seen from within" (Alhambra). Manueline puts carving on portals and windows: Jerónimos' 32 x 12 m south portal on a long plain facade; Tomar's window. Spend polygons on silhouettes and texture budget on interiors and thresholds.
2. **One module at real size, arrayed.** Segovia is 167 arches of two sizes (5.10 and about 4.50 m); the Atarazanas are 17 identical naves; the Court of the Lions is 17 x 11 bays in a 1-2-3 column rhythm; Palma is a comb of buttresses. Build one bay and vary the rhythm, not the detail.
3. **Three-band walls.** A tile dado, a stucco, sebka or sgraffito field, then a wooden or muqarnas ceiling or tiled eaves. All three are textures; only arches, column shafts and eaves edges are geometry.
4. **Keep silhouette features as geometry; make lace an alpha cut-out.** That means:
   - pyramidal against square merlons, round bartizans and slate cones;
   - conical chimneys (33 m, Sintra), openwork spires (88 m, Burgos) and stacked belfries (Giralda);
   - flying buttresses, cantilevered timber balconies, rock overhangs and the dark voids of bridge arches.
5. **Bake light and water into vertex colour.**
   - Dark arch soffits and vault crowns; damp bases; soiled whitewash.
   - Pools of light under star-shaped bath skylights; baked reflections in pools and cisterns.
   - A fog-filled gorge fakes 100 m of depth.
6. **Let materials name the district** (9.9), and keep true scale where the sources give it. A 0.75 m passage (Coimbra), a 1 m cliff path (Caminito) and a 26 m moat (Segovia) are gameplay facts, not decoration.

San Juan de Gaztelugatxe was Dragonstone in season 7, filmed July-October 2016: a chapel on an islet 80 m above sea level, reached by 241 steps over a stone bridge ([en.wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_Gaztelugatxe); [image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gaztelugatxe_en_espagne.jpg)).

  - Peñíscola was Meereen in season 6.
  - Girona was Braavos and part of King's Landing.
  - The Castle of Zafra was the Tower of Joy ([image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castillo_de_Zafra,_Campillo_de_Dueñas,_Guadalajara,_España,_2017-01-04,_DD_41-46_PAN.jpg)).
  - Almodóvar del Río was reportedly Highgarden.
- **Takeaway:** the show's "maximal" Spanish sets are real buildings whose richness is concentrated (courtyards, gardens, vaults), set in plain massive envelopes. That is the "controlled" half of the brief.
