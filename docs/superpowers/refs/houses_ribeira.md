# The Ribeira's houses: references (Oct 2 2026)

Gathered for the harbour's art pass (the user: the windows and verandas
should feel more real, the roofs aren't solid; a good first pass lacking
detail). Mostly from J. Teixeira's FAUP thesis on the Porto "casa burguesa"
(2004, after Veiga de Oliveira and Galhano), FEUP/FAUP rehabilitation theses
and SIPA's record for Praca da Ribeira; "photo est." is read from photographs.

Period: sash windows came only after 1703, iron railings in the 18th
century, parapets late 18th, azulejo fronts and Marseille-tile gables mid to
late 19th. For the 16th-17th century: timber balconies (posts and a handrail)
or early wrought iron, lattice shutters (rotulas, hinged at the lintel, opening
out), plain casements, low hipped canal-tile roofs. The game's city mixes
periods by design; the details below are what reads real.

## Fronts

Plots 3-6 m wide (c.4.5 m), 10-15 m deep; 2-4 storeys (later 5-7), 3.2-3.6 m
floor to floor, a taller shop floor. Granite front walls 30-70 cm, party walls
c.40 cm. Openings c.1.25 m wide (a full-height balcony door 1.33 x 2.87 m).

Ranked: (1) reveals shallow outside, deep inside: of a 60-70 cm wall c.20 cm is
the window frame and 40-50 cm the inner folding shutters (portadas, 3 leaves);
from the street the glass sits only 10-15 cm back, the deep inner reveal with
white panelled shutters folded into it; (2) granite surrounds 20-25 cm wide,
c.3 cm proud of the render, each jamb one stone through the wall, a two-stone
lintel, a one-stone sill; many Ribeira heads segmental or round with a
keystone; (3) glazing: sashes 3-4 panes by 2-3 in a c.6 cm box; balcony doors
side-hung, two leaves, glazing bars, panelled bottoms, a fixed (often fan or
arched) transom light; sashes white, frames the door colour; (4) composition:
two openings against the party walls with a blank pier between (a round window,
a shrine, arms) or three evenly spaced; (5) granite quoin and pilaster strips
at each party wall, a granite ground floor with wide shop doors; (6) doors:
one heavy panelled leaf (older) or two narrow leaves with barred panels and a
barred transom, iron pins leaded into the granite; dark green, red, blue,
brown, black; (7) bands: balcony slabs or moulded ledges at each floor, a
granite cornice (cimalha) under the eaves; on timber fronts rafter ends with
rounded tips; (8) timber-framed upper floors projecting over stone (andares em
ressalto), slate or tile clad; (9) originally no gutters (stone spouts);
later zinc gutters and c.75 mm downpipes kinking round the balcony slabs;
(10) azulejos 14-15.5 cm with border tiles, stopping at the surrounds.

## Balconies (varandas)

(1) One granite slab 15-20 cm thick, projecting no more than c.0.5 m on street
fronts (17th-18th c.: on granite corbels; 19th: cantilevered, no corbels; rear
balconies c.1.2 m on big corbels); (2) corbels (cachorros): low and wide ending
in a scroll curling down and out (commonest, 17th c.), or a wedge with a curved
face; or a continuous heavy moulded ledge; (3) a moulded front edge (bullnose
or ogee), reading c.20 cm from below; (4) running balconies across the whole
front (varanda corrida) or one per balcony door; in the Ribeira running ones
repeat every floor; (5) railings: wood (17th), wrought iron (18th), cast panels
(19th); corner finials ("gilded pyramids", 1788); (6) railings 0.885-0.98 m high,
gaps 108-120 mm, bars c.16-20 mm, flat top and bottom rails, a band of circles
or S-scrolls, returns to the wall at each end; (7) lower window guards of the
same pattern across sill-height windows; (8) closed-in timber-and-glass
galleries at the back, glazed lookouts (mirantes) on the roofs.

## Roofs

(1) Until the mid-19th century a low four-pitched canal-tile roof: the main
slopes fall onto the party walls, the hips (tacanicas) face the street and the
back, the ridge runs front to back; 20-25 deg (the ridge c.1 m over the eave on
a 5 m house); from above the street is a comb of long narrow hips with gutters
between; (2) party-wall gutters (algerozes) out to the street by spouts;
(3) eaves (beirado): 2-3 overlapping rows of canal tile, each stepping further
out on a granite cornice (or a timber entablature with rafter ends), the first
row of big tiles (c.80 cm), projecting c.0.4-0.6 m; (4) glazed skylights over
stairwells (a small dome on a drum; square lights in the slope); (5) dormers
(trapeiras) and glazed lookout boxes (mirantes); (6) chimneys not
characteristic (plain rendered stacks at the rear, later); (7) later: Marseille
tile two-pitch roofs with tall party-wall gables; balustraded parapets.

Why roofs read as thin planes, and the fix: a stacked eave edge (a cornice
block 25-40 cm tall projecting 15-30 cm, then 2-3 tile rows stepping out
10-15 cm each); the round tile mouths (c.15 cm) along the eave and the hips; a
visible eave underside; tile courses with real corrugation in silhouette
(20-25 cm pitch, 6-8 cm relief); a ridge roll 15-20 cm; never a plane ending
in a knife edge (always against a party-wall coping or gutter).

## The arcade and the quay wall

The Muro dos Bacalhoeiros: the 14th-century Fernandine wall (c.9 m,
crenellated), now a quay wall with houses on it; round arches cut through it
(18th-19th c.), once separated by small round windows (photo est.: wall
4.5-5.5 m over the quay, a c.1 m parapet; rough ashlar courses 0.45-0.5 m,
blocks 0.8-1.2 m; arches 1.5-2.2 m wide, 2.6-3.5 m to the crown, on 3-3.5 m
centres; a plain ring of radial stones 35-45 cm; iron-barred gates; small
square vents between the arches). Praca da Ribeira: arcades after a fire
c.1491, remodelled 1776-82 (an arcaded gallery, a principal floor of tall
balconied openings, small sill-height windows).
