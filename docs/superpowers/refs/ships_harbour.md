# Ships of the harbour: references (Oct 2 2026)

Gathered for the harbour's art pass (the user: the ships "feel like made out of
paper"; grander, more complex, filled out, from real references). Figures from
the sources unless marked est.; the Mary Rose (1545) is the one surviving hull
of the period, the Portuguese nau's numbers mostly the Pepper Wreck (1606, F.
Castro's reconstruction from 16th-century treatises).

## The carrack / nau (c.1500-1550)

- Mary Rose: keel c.32 m, beam c.12 m, draught 4.6 m; four levels; castles of
  2-3 decks. Santa Catarina do Monte Sinai (c.1520): 38 m x 13 m, draught
  4-4.5 m. Pepper Wreck (600 t): keel 27.72 m, overall c.39.3 m, beam 12.32 m,
  flat of floor 4.11 m, depth c.9 m. Nao Victoria replica: 25.9 x 6.72 m.
- Proportion: beam 1 : keel 2 : length 3 (length/beam 2.9-3.4). A 4:1 hull
  reads as a caravel or galleon.
- Hero nau: c.38 m overall, 27 m keel, 12 m beam, 4.5-5 m draught; waist rail
  4-5 m over the water; castles 2-3 decks of 2-2.3 m (aftercastle top 11-13 m).

Ranked: (1) volume and tumblehome (near-round sections, widest at the
waterline, castles narrower than the beam); (2) strong sheer to both ends;
(3) forecastle triangular in plan, 2-3 decks, overhanging the stem on knees
(no beakhead before c.1550); (4) 2-4 wales, often paired, 25-35 cm tall,
10-15 cm proud, converging fore and aft; (5) beam ends through the planking
and vertical skids/riders over the wales (the carrack's signature: Mataro
model, Master W.A.); (6) aftercastle stepped up aft, a plain transom with a
helm port and small windows, no stern galleries before c.1560-80; (7) gunports
0.5-0.7 m, lids 10-12 cm on two iron straps (Mary Rose main deck 7-8 a side,
c.40 cm over the water; castle swivel ports); (8) deadeyes outboard on the
upper wale or a channel board 0.4-0.6 m, chainplates down over the wales;
(9) rudder of vertical planks bound in iron, 5-6 pintle/gudgeon pairs, straps
0.6-1.2 m; (10) waist: hatches with 20-30 cm coamings, the ship's boat (8-10 m)
on them, bitts (no belaying-pin rows); (11) windlass under the forecastle, a
capstan aft, two hawse holes a side, anchors at the bow on a davit (a 7 m
anchor at the Mary Rose site); (12) castle bulwarks of open stanchions under a
cap rail hung with pavises (painted shields); (13) 1-3 big stern lanterns
(c.1 m); (14) colours: tarred black-brown hull, ochre/natural castles, painted
bands, the red Cross of the Order of Christ on the sails, an off-white bottom.

## Its rig

Fore and main square with topsails, lateen mizzen (the Mary Rose a fourth,
the bonaventure), bowsprit and spritsail. Mainmast (Pepper Wreck) 31.68 m,
about 2.6 x beam; fore c.4/5 of main; topmasts c.1/2 the lower; main yard c.5/6
of the keel (c.23 m); mainmast 70-83 cm at its foot.

Ranked: (1) a huge round tub-shaped top, 3-4 m across, sides 0.8-1 m (planked
or wicker), hung with pavises, a lubber's hole; (2) shrouds main 7-10 a side,
fore 5-7, mizzen 3-4; (3) ratlines 38-46 cm apart (one alpha-card net);
(4) chunky dark deadeyes 25-35 cm, lanyards; (5) a very thick mainstay to the
stem (15+ cm), forestay to the bowsprit; (6) lifts from masthead to yardarms
(triangles in silhouette); (7) furled courses as big lashed bundles 0.5-0.8 m
on the yards; (8) the main tie and its tackle beside the mast; (9) braces aft,
bowlines forward; (10) crowsfeet fanning from the top; (11) the lateen mizzen
yard as long as its mast, raked steeply; (12) the spritsail yard under the
bowsprit.

## The caravel

Vera Cruz / Boa Esperanca replicas: 23.8 m x 6.6 m, draught 3.3 m; mainmast
18 m with a 26 m yard (longer than the hull), mizzen 16 m with a 20 m yard;
lateen sails with the Cross of Christ. Ranked: yards longer than the hull,
raked c.45 deg high end aft, thick where two spars are lashed; no forecastle,
an open low bow; one low raised deck aft (the tolda, 1.5-2 m) over the tiller;
low freeboard, fine entry; heavy parrel and halyard tackle; mast raked
forward, few shrouds set up with tackles; the furled lateen along its yard;
lighter wales and cap rail.

## Fishing and river boats

For the 16th century: the small lateen fishing caravel and open boats
(bateis). Later types for form: the meia-lua (8.5 x 2.4 x 0.8 m, a crescent
sheer, tall pointed bow) and the Douro rabelo (19-23 m, flat-bottomed,
clinker, a long stern oar from a raised platform). Ranked: crescent sheer;
clinker laps; thwarts, frames and inside planking modelled; thole pins and
heavy oars; a proud stem post; painted bands, eyes or crosses; nets, baskets
and tubs aboard; wet dark bottoms, worn gunwales.

## What reads solid, not paper

(1) Real thickness at every visible edge: planking 11 cm, frames 22-25 cm,
bulwarks 20-30 cm with an overhanging cap rail 15-25 cm, never single-sided;
(2) the bulwarks' inside: stanchions/frame heads every 50-60 cm, ceiling
planking, the waterway; (3) wales 10-15 cm proud, broken by beam ends and
skids; (4) the full hull under the waterline (4.5-5 m) with a wet band;
(5) deck camber (20-25 cm on 12 m) and sheer; (6) mass in the masts (70-80 cm),
woldings, caps, cheeks, the top's supports; (7) a hierarchy of rope sizes
(stays 15+ cm, shrouds 5-7 cm, running 2-4 cm); (8) 60-100 lines on the nau,
ratline nets and crowsfeet doing most of it; (9) iron: chains, straps,
hinges, rings, bolt heads; (10) weathering: tar streaks from scuppers and
chainplates, salt on the rails, grime at the waterline; (11) clutter: barrels,
rope, the boat, pavises, flags; (12) silhouette layers: castles, tops,
furled sails and rigging fans against the sky.
