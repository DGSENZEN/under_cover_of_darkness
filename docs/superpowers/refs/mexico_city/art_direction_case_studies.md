# Art Direction and World-Building Case Studies for an Alternate-Universe 1920s Mexico City

Scope: how Grim Fandango, Deadlock, Dishonored 1 and 2 and other works built their look and world, reduced to methods a small team can reuse for a first-person stealth/investigation game in a gothic, folkloric, alternate 1920s Mexico City (Godot 4, stylized PS2-era look).

Research date: 7 October 2026. Some fetches failed (Eurogamer, Vice/Motherboard, Frieze, Dot Esports, PC Gamer article bodies, ArtStation all returned errors or paywalls). Where a claim comes only from a search-engine summary of a page I could not open, it is marked "(search summary, page not opened)".

## Grim Fandango (1998): how Mexican folklore, Art Deco and film noir were combined

### Takeaway
Grim Fandango works because two unrelated sources were set against each other and held to one rule each. Day of the Dead and Aztec underworld folklore gave the characters and the cosmology. 1930s noir and Mayan-Revival Art Deco gave the tone, the architecture and the way people talk. A four-year, four-location structure gave each district its own act, look and puzzle set. Schafer has said openly that the pairing started as an accident of what the team was into, and that the folklore was adapted rather than reproduced faithfully.

### Cited Findings
**Origins and how the sources were mixed**
- Schafer first met Posada-style calavera art and the Day of the Dead in a UC Berkeley folklore class taught by Alan Dundes. — [Kill Screen, "Exhuming Grim Fandango's Mexican folklore inspirations"](https://www.killscreen.com/exhuming-grim-fandangos-mexican-folklore-inspirations/)
- Schafer on how noir and folklore came together: two ideas "crash into each other and you'll realize that they accentuate each other. That's what happened with Grim." — [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/exhuming-grim-fandangos-mexican-folklore-inspirations/)
- Schafer: "We loved the art and folklore of the Day of the Dead, and I'd studied some anthropology at college, and I was also reading a lot of Raymond Chandler." Noir suited the setting because it is "the afterlife, where you still have troubles and corruption and crime." The team chose to "look into cultures that are not usually represented in games, and to juxtapose these two things that maybe shouldn't go together." — [Vice, Tim Schafer discusses Grim Fandango and Monkey Island](https://vice.com/en/article/tim-schafer-discusses-grim-fandango-and-monkey-island-990)
- The game reworks the Aztec soul's journey to Mictlán (nine levels) into a four-year walk. Virtuous souls ride the Number Nine train, which arrives in four minutes, and everyone else walks with a cane topped by a compass. Kill Screen points out that Aztec afterlife placement actually depended on how and in what social role a person died, not on morality, so the game's merit-based version is more European than Aztec. Schafer said he wanted to capture "the themes and feelings of it," not only specific details. — [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/exhuming-grim-fandangos-mexican-folklore-inspirations/)
- One named architectural source is 450 Sutter Street in San Francisco, described as "San Francisco's monument to the Mayan Revival branch of Art Deco." — [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/exhuming-grim-fandangos-mexican-folklore-inspirations/); also cited on [Wikipedia, Peter Chan (artist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Chan_(artist))
- The look combines 1930s Art Deco skyscrapers with Aztec temple architecture. Most characters are calaca skeletons drawn from Posada and Day of the Dead figures, and demons such as Glottis borrow from Ed "Big Daddy" Roth's hot-rod art. — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)
- Named noir sources: *Casablanca* (two characters modelled on the Peter Lorre and Claude Rains roles, and the villain Hector LeMans on Sydney Greenstreet), *The Maltese Falcon*, *Double Indemnity*, *Chinatown*, *Key Largo*, *The Third Man* and *Glengarry Glen Ross*. Schafer: "there's something that I feel is really honest about the way people talked that's different than modern movies." — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)
- Peter Chan drew on Mayan and Mexican motifs and architecture and paired them with Art Deco and noir. A review compares the first Manny and Meche scene to an exchange from *Double Indemnity* or *The Big Sleep*. — [Slant Magazine, Grim Fandango Remastered review](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/review-grim-fandango-remastered) (search summary, page not opened)

**Peter Chan's concept art and the technical approach**
- Chan worked freelance and remotely as the backgrounds artist, posting stacks of concept art every week. Schafer described the packages arriving as exciting presents. Chan's goal was film work, and he drew Grim Fandango's art with that in mind, so it differs from typical game concept art. — [Wikipedia, Peter Chan (artist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Chan_(artist)); [MobyGames credits](https://www.mobygames.com/person/1165/)
- Backgrounds were pre-rendered from 3D models, which allowed free camera framing without redrawing. Manny is about 250 polygons. There are more than 90 sets and about 50 characters. Manny's head turns towards nearby interactive objects to hint at them. — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)

**Structure: four years, four locations**
- Each of the four acts takes place on the Day of the Dead, one year apart: (1) El Marrow and the Department of Death bureaucracy; (2) Rubacava, a port city at the edge of the Petrified Forest; (3) the Edge of the World, a coral-mining operation; (4) Puerta del Sol, the terminus at the Ninth Underworld. — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)

**Puzzle and dialogue design**
- Each year contains non-linear puzzle paths, mapped as prerequisite dependencies in puzzle design documents. Time pressure forced cuts, including a five-step final puzzle against Hector. — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)
- The puzzle design document dates from April 1996 and was written by Schafer, Peter Tsacle, Eric Ingerson, Bret Mogilefsky and Peter Chan. The Video Game History Foundation describes it as illustrated and explaining "the characters & puzzle structure." — [Video Game History Foundation archive page](https://gamehistory.org/?p=1594)
- Schafer published the document as a 72-page PDF, including early art and cut content. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/schafer-releases-i-grim-fandango-i-puzzle-document) (search summary, page not opened)
- Schafer's puzzle philosophy: keep players entertained while they are stuck, and deliberately offer the obvious wrong answer and "make sure that wouldn't work," so the real solution lands as an "aha." Dialogue was tested in a group, much like a television comedy writers' room. — [Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/tim-schafer-discusses-grim-fandango-and-monkey-island-990)

**The Remastered edition and its commentary**
- Grim Fandango Remastered (Double Fine, 2015) includes almost two hours of developer commentary. Schafer, Peter Chan and composer Peter McConnell also spoke on a PAX Prime 2014 panel. The remaster went back to the original uncompressed assets. — [PAX Prime 2014 panel notes, Unofficial Convention Archive](https://www.theunofficialconventionarchive.com/pax-prime-2014-grim-fandango-bringing-the-dead-back-to-life/); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/grim_fandango/b/playstation4/archive/2014/08/30/tim-schafer-isn-t-completely-sure-why-double-fine-decided-to-remaster-grim-fandango.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Transferable method (collision of two sources):** pick one folk or religious source and one period-genre source, then give each a job. In Grim Fandango, folklore supplies who people are and how the cosmos works, while noir and Deco supply tone, buildings and speech. For 1920s CDMX an equivalent split could be Posada, Day of the Dead, Aztec cosmology and colonial Catholic gothic for *beings and cosmology*, with Mexican 1920s Art Deco, Neocolonial and Mayan Revival plus noir and cabaret for *architecture, institutions and speech*.
- **Real Mayan-Revival Deco gives the setting authenticity for free.** The style Grim borrowed from San Francisco also existed in real Mexico City during the 1920s and 30s, so a CDMX game can use the local originals instead of an American copy.
- **One district per act** (El Marrow, Rubacava, Edge of the World, Puerta del Sol), each with its own industry (bureaucracy, port and gambling, mining, terminus), is a strong template for a compact game. Each district needs a work it does, a vice and a palette.
- **Puzzle dependency charts** like the 1996 document suit an investigation game. Write each district's puzzles and dialogue gates as a dependency graph before building the space.
- Grim uses Aztec cosmology loosely, and Kill Screen says so. A CDMX project made with a Mexican audience in mind should decide deliberately where it is loose and where it is faithful.

### Gaps
- I found no published standalone art book of Peter Chan's Grim Fandango art. The Remastered release ships a concept art viewer and commentary inside the game. The task's "The Art of Grim Fandango Remastered" could not be confirmed as a real book.
- I could not open a transcript of the Remastered commentary. Its specific remarks on Rubacava's design, the papier-mâché look of the characters and Glottis's design remain unsourced here.
- I found no GDC postmortem for Grim Fandango.

## Deadlock (Valve): setting, Patrons, art direction and its "niche sauce"

### Takeaway
Deadlock is a playtest, not a released game. Valve's Steam page still says it is in "early development with lots of temporary art," and the store gives no official setting description. Almost all detailed lore comes from in-game voice lines, the visual novels and fan wiki curation. The art direction is an occult, alternate New York (fans call it "the Cursed Apple"), split into an upper-class neoclassical Uptown and a working-class brick Downtown. Two Patron gods are carried by effigies. Valve has said little about its methods, so treat any claimed "Valve art principles" as analysis unless a Valve source is named.

### Cited Findings
**Official status and dates**
- Valve's Steam page says Deadlock "is in early development with lots of temporary art and experimental gameplay," that access is by friend invite through playtesters, and that the release date is "To be announced." It describes no setting. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1422450/Deadlock/)
- Timeline: development reportedly began in 2018. "Neon Prime" was trademarked in October 2022. The game leaked in May 2024, revealing a move from sci-fi/cyberpunk (internally Japan-inspired) to fantasy/steampunk. The Steam page went up and the NDA lifted in August 2024. A February 2025 update cut the lanes from four to three. Wikipedia also lists an August 2025 visual-style overhaul. — [Wikipedia, Deadlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlock_(video_game)); [The Verge hands-on, Aug 2024](https://www.theverge.com/2024/8/12/24219016/valve-deadlock-hands-on-secret-new-game)
- The "City Never Sleeps" update (about 1-2 October 2026) adds six heroes: Nurse Harrow, Rat King, Solomon, Deadman Danny, Baba and Violet. — [GamingOnLinux, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/valves-deadlock-gets-a-massive-update-still-a-while-away-from-a-public-release/); [OpenCritic/TheGamer, 2 Oct 2026](https://opencritic.com/news/36999/deadlock-city-never-sleeps-adds-6-new-heroes-updated-visuals-and-more). Wikipedia dates it September 2026; the news articles put it at 1-2 October 2026.
- Per a search summary, PC Gamer reports that City Never Sleeps redecorates the whole map into three themes: "a bright city center, a haunted university, and a shadowy stock exchange." Buildings and creeps are redesigned to match, including pickle jars with hands growing out of them, sentient stacks of books and slimy trash-bag monsters. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/moba/deadlocks-gigantic-new-update-gives-its-city-a-spooky-new-coat-of-paint-and-debuts-6-new-heroes-including-the-one-and-only-rat-king/) (search summary, page body blocked)
- Valve's first development update: "There is still a good amount of work we wanted to do on the core game itself before we moved onto more polish phases." It also plans gradual hero remodels and updates to the Midboss, Guardians and Walkers. — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/valves-deadlock-gets-a-massive-update-still-a-while-away-from-a-public-release/); [PC Gamer, "still too unfinished"](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/moba/valve-says-deadlock-is-still-too-unfinished-to-release-publicly-but-has-hero-cosmetics-and-new-game-modes-in-the-works/)

**Setting and lore (mostly community-curated from in-game sources)**
- The wiki dates the setting to about the 1930s-1940s. The architecture reflects New York City up to 1932, while some characters are deliberately anachronistic: Shiv has a 1950s greaser look, Billy a 1970s punk look, and one computer resembles the 1973 Xerox Alto. The wiki says Valve has published no full lore document. — [Deadlock Wiki, Storyline](https://deadlock.wiki/Storyline)
- Wikipedia calls the setting an alternate *1940s* New York nicknamed "the Cursed Apple," which conflicts with "early 20th century" descriptions elsewhere. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlock_(video_game)); compare [Deadlock Wiki](https://deadlock.wiki/Storyline)
- The Maelstrom, a supernatural cataclysm 50 years before the ritual's "Year 0," revealed the occult to the world. Examples: Vindicta returned as a ghost of a 1692 Salem witch-trial victim, and Lady Geist made a pact with Oathkeeper. The Baxter Society hunts the supernatural. The New York Oracle is an in-world newspaper and broadcaster. — [Deadlock Wiki, Storyline](https://deadlock.wiki/Storyline); [Deadlock Wiki, The New York Oracle](https://deadlock.wiki/The_New_York_Oracle)
- The Patrons are extraplanar elder gods. The Hidden King is a shadowy antlered figure with yellow eyes, bound to a burning wicker-man effigy, whose base is a factory full of speakers and microphones; his side of the map is working-class red brick, labelled "Downtown." The Archmother wears a broken mask shaped like the Statue of Liberty's head, is bound to a crystalline effigy with blue flame, and her base is a neoclassical museum; her side is upper-class white brick, labelled "Uptown." — [Deadlock Wiki, Hidden King](https://deadlock.wiki/Hidden_King)
- Per a search summary, the Hidden King was drawn from Robert W. Chambers' *The King in Yellow*. — [Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=1169578) (search summary only). I could not confirm this from a Valve source; it should be treated as analysis.

**Art direction: what has been said**
- Press compares the look to Half-Life 2's City 17, *BioShock Infinite* and Tim Burton, and describes it as "alternative-world fantasy goth New York." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlock_(video_game))
- Community discussion praises a coherent Art Deco/steampunk/Prohibition roster with distinct silhouettes and typographic hero logos, and debates whether a painterly look survives in 3D. — [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/is-this-the-slickest-looking-character-roster-in-a-new-video-game-in-a-while.1430845/); [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1422450/discussions/0/4760955347172943856)
- Before release, leaked visuals were mocked ("WTF is this art style"), a reminder that playtest art is temporary. — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/wtf-art-style-leaked-visuals-valve-s-upcoming-title-deadlock-fail-impress-fans)
- **Trap for anyone researching this:** ArtStation results for "Deadlock concept" are often Riot's *VALORANT* agent named Deadlock (for example the Gary Tanaka and Viviane Herzog posts), not Valve's game. — [ArtStation, VALORANT Deadlock concepts](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/RyBPKW). A post by Evgeniy Evstratiy, "deadlock (alpha) - posters," may be Valve work, but the page returned 403 and I could not verify it. — [ArtStation](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/kN9Bax)

### Inferences
- **Deadlock's "niche sauce" (my synthesis, not Valve's statement).** The ingredients are: (a) one real, specific city frozen at a cut-off year (NYC up to 1932); (b) a single historical rupture, the Maelstrom, that makes the occult public and ordinary; (c) gods embodied as civic monuments (Liberty-mask Archmother, wicker-man Hidden King) mapped onto real class geography (Uptown white stone against Downtown red brick); (d) heroes allowed to be anachronistic pastiche, reading as "American genre crossroads" rather than strict period costume; (e) mundane objects turned uncanny, such as pickle jars with hands and sentient books.
- **For 1920s CDMX:** a Patron-style pairing could follow real class geography, for example Porfirian or French-style Colonia Roma and Juárez mansions against the tenement vecindades of Tepito and La Merced. The patrons' effigies could twist national monuments, such as the Ángel de la Independencia or Palacio de Bellas Artes iconography, the way the Archmother twists Liberty.
- **District themes as monster themes.** City Never Sleeps reportedly gives each district its own creature family (university = books, stock exchange = shadow). The same method works for a stealth game: each barrio's ambient "uncanny" props and spirit creatures come from that barrio's work.
- Deadlock is still changing; Valve plans hero remodels. Use it for direction and mood, not as a fixed spec.

### Gaps
- I found no credited Valve artist interview, GDC talk or Valve-authored art-direction statement for Deadlock. "Silhouettes, painterly textures, occult-tech fusion, Art Nouveau/Deco" is supported only by community and press analysis.
- I could not open the PC Gamer article bodies or Eurogamer, so district-theme details rest on search summaries.
- I could not verify the King in Yellow link from a primary source.

## Dishonored 1 and 2 (Arkane; Viktor Antonov, Sébastien Mitton): building a "familiar but other" city

### Takeaway
Arkane's method is research first, twist second. The team made photo trips to real cities, studied faces, mug shots and anatomy, went to paintings and sculpture rather than other games, and invented a world history before modelling anything. Each city was then built around one invented economy (whale oil in Dunwall, silver mining and dust in Karnaca) that explains its tech, its social divisions and its stealth spaces. Karnaca is the closest analogue to a Latin-American city. It mixes Larnaca, Greece, Italy and Spain with Cuban reference photos, and the team researched it in Barcelona because Cuba was not possible.

### Cited Findings
**Dunwall (Dishonored, 2012)**
- Antonov and Mitton studied the architecture of London and Edinburgh and worked with an anatomist on turn-of-the-century British physiognomy. Antonov says the game was influenced by history, art and anatomy rather than games or films, and that "everything is a character." — [PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dishonored-preview-power-possession-and-pacifism-in-a-hand-crafted-world)
- Antonov: "This is a steampunk Victorian world, but it has no rivets, no brass, no copper, and no tubes." Mitton: "The place is divided and controlled by fences. The parks are fenced off." To the French team, London felt oppressive: a maze of barriers and walls covered in propaganda. Named references include *Brazil*, *Metropolis*, *Blade Runner* and BioShock. The team built a whole continental backstory, including accents, while building only the parts the player sees. — [GamesBeat, "How Dishonored's artists created an oppressive… world from scratch"](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Mitton: the first idea was 1666 London, whose skyline then had "more than 80 cathedrals." Plague imagery came from Black Plague testimonies, and Christo and Jeanne-Claude inspired the lime-washed mass graves. Character faces resulted from "photo trips to London and Edinburgh, analysis of mug-shots and studies of typical English traits." A cut asylum level had sound-sensitive "Lunatics." The Tallboy began when Mitton saw a stilt-walking window cleaner in Austin; it went from town crier to armed guard carrying whale-oil canisters. — [Kotaku, Dishonored's art director on inspiration and cut levels](https://kotaku.com/dishonoreds-art-director-talks-inspiration-cut-levels-5953087)
- At GDC 2013 Mitton said Dishonored was nearly set in medieval Japan but "We thought medieval Japan was not good for us because we do not know this culture." He described London photo and face-reference trips and a long concept phase before any 3D work. — [Game Informer, "Dishonored almost took place in Japan"](https://Www.Gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/03/28/dishonored-almost-took-place-in-japan.aspx); [Destructoid GDC coverage](https://www.destructoid.com/?p=122544)
- Antonov also stressed defending art precision with the publisher and designers. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dishonored-preview-power-possession-and-pacifism-in-a-hand-crafted-world)

**Karnaca (Dishonored 2, 2016)**
- Karnaca is "based on the Cypriot city of Larnaca (hence its name) with inspiration from… Greece, Italy and Spain" (Game Informer, issue 304). Reference photos came from "Cuba, Lyon and Malibu, California." The art drew on "the architectural forms, popular fashions, and far-out technologies" of 1851. Arkane used paintings and sculpture, and Lucie Minne sculpted clay busts. Writing Karnaca's history reportedly took about a year (Gameplanet interview with Mitton). — [Wikipedia, Dishonored 2 (development section and its citations)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonored_2)
- Mitton went to Barcelona and sketched in the La Rambla quarter, with its "narrow streets and flat roofs." The windbreaker concept was born there: "I was actually walking around the quarter of La Rambla… I was sketching it down, put the first concept of a windbreaker on it — the idea was born." The city's logic: a mountain divides rich from poor, silver mines create dust storms that hit the lower quarters, and windbreakers protect the wealthy areas. Characters look weathered by dust rather than "clean and shiny." Mitton: "It's a combination of everything you can see and feel and smell"; he likes talking to "carpenters or gardeners." — [Inverse interview with Sébastien Mitton](https://www.inverse.com/article/23269-dishonored-2-sebastien-mitton-art-director-arkane-studios-interview)
- Per search summaries: the art brief named "Havana or Genoa." Mitton chose Barcelona because he could not travel to Cuba and called Barcelona "like Havana, but in a better state." The team mixed a British curiosity cabinet with a rich Cuban or Italian interior, and Spanish siesta street life (card games, long lunches in the street) shaped Karnaca's street life. — [Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/?p=39301); [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/dishonored-2/dishonored-2-waves-goodbye-to-dunwall-welcome-to-karnaca) (search summaries; I did not open these pages to confirm the exact quotes)
- Mitton values fine artists and painters for concept work, which gives Dishonored 2 its painterly quality. "Art is a combination of everything you see, smell, eat and feel." — [Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/23269-dishonored-2-sebastien-mitton-art-director-arkane-studios-interview); see also [80.lv, How Arkane hires artists](https://80.lv/articles/how-does-arkane-studios-hire-artists)
- Art books: *The Art of Dishonored 2* (Dark Horse, 15 November 2016), with Mitton's commentary on character, weapon, prop and environment concepts. — [Dishonored Wiki entry](https://dishonored.fandom.com/wiki/The_Art_of_Dishonored_2); [Parka Blogs review with page photos](https://www.parkablogs.com/content/book-review-art-of-dishonored-2). An official online gallery also exists: [Bethesda, Karnaca art gallery](https://bethesda.net/en/article/3VITzlfbNCm4gks4w8MA0a/dishonored-2-karnaca-art-gallery)

**Level design and readability**
- Dishonored's levels are described as dense, mostly wide-linear with some hubs, with many routes, rooftop and window-to-window paths, and visual landmarks to help orientation. — [GameBanshee review](https://www.gamebanshee.com/x886); [Ulster University student paper, Level Design Techniques in Dishonored 2 (PDF)](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00860009-fad/files/2023/02/Level-Design-Techniques-in-Dishonored-2.pdf) (secondary/academic-student source)

### Inferences
- **The "one invented economy" rule.** Dunwall runs on whale oil, which explains Tallboys, canisters, walls of light and the whaling-ship economy. Karnaca runs on silver mining and dust, which explains windbreakers, the class split by altitude and weathered faces. For 1920s CDMX, pick one invented resource that bends the real city. Candidates rooted in the real Valley of Mexico: drained-lake silt and subsidence, pulque, Hidalgo silver (also the setting of *Mexican Gothic*), volcanic tezontle stone, or an occult version of the Desagüe drainage works. Let that one resource explain the tech, the class geography and the stealth spaces (canals, drainage tunnels, rooftop azoteas).
- **Do not build a culture you do not know.** Mitton's Japan remark and Gutiérrez's "I am Mexico" stance (below) point the same way. A non-Mexican team should hire or consult Mexican artists and writers, as Arkane hired specialists (an anatomist, sculptors).
- **Research a stand-in city when the real one is out of reach,** as Barcelona stood in for Havana. A small team can do the same from historic photo archives such as Casasola-era Mexico City photography, with Puebla or Guanajuato as living stand-ins.
- **Faces from local physiognomy.** Arkane studied mug shots and regional traits; 1920s Mexican police and studio photo archives offer the same for NPC heads.
- **Fences and divisions make stealth.** "The place is divided and controlled by fences" is both a theme and a level-design tool: barriers create guarded thresholds, and rooftops create the alternative route.
- **Settings take time.** Karnaca's history reportedly took about a year to write. Budget lore time before blockout.

## Other references: what to take from each

### Takeaway
Each secondary reference contributes one specific tool. Del Toro gives colour coding by meaning and "eye protein" sets. Gutiérrez gives a handmade folk-art material and an insider's (not a tourist's) Mexico. Coco gives history stacked vertically and skeleton expression rules. Blasphemous shows Catholic art treated as popular culture. Thief shows overlapping eras in one city. Pentiment and Disco Elysium show dialogue that carries investigation and character. Kentucky Route Zero shows how to play magical realism straight. Mignola shows lucha libre and Aztec pulp. *Mexican Gothic* shows foreign colonial extraction as the real horror.

### Cited Findings
**Guillermo del Toro (Cronos, The Devil's Backbone, Pan's Labyrinth, Crimson Peak)**
- He colour-codes: in *Hellboy*, red was used only for things connected to Hellboy. In *The Devil's Backbone*, amber links the fetuses in jars, the ghost and an insect in amber, all symbols of a ghost as "a moment suspended in time." In *Crimson Peak*, ivory and gold (America, sunlight, money) are set against cyan and blue (old Europe). — [DGA Quarterly interview with del Toro, Winter 2014](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/1401-winter-2014/dga-interview-del-toro)
- Every set should make "one statement" and be "readable quickly": the Pale Man's chimney and table, the gears in the Captain's office, the bomb in the courtyard. "Eye candy… looks good but it doesn't tell the story. Eye protein is beautiful and technically complex, but it tells the story." "There is a heavy Mexican Catholic streak in my movies, and a huge Mexican sense of melodrama." — [DGA Quarterly](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/1401-winter-2014/dga-interview-del-toro)
- He writes colour-palette memos per film. *Devil's Backbone* uses desaturated orange days and ghostly blue nights. — [FilmLifestyle](https://filmlifestyle.com/guillermo-del-toro-color-palette/) (secondary); [Criterion, Cronos](https://www.criterion.com/films/27534-cronos)

**Jorge R. Gutiérrez (The Book of Life, El Tigre)**
- He wanted the world to feel handmade, taken from Mexican folk-art toys: "art by the people for the people… a mirror of who we are in Mexico." Artisans hand-carved wooden puppets of the characters, which were then translated into 3D, keeping marionette joints, rusted metal and chipped paint. The film grew from his CalArts thesis *Carmelo* (2001). He co-designed with Sandra Equihua. — [MPA, "Spirits & Passion Collide in The Book of Life"](https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/10/spirits-passion-collide-in-the-book-of-life/); [AWN interview](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/jorge-gutierrez-talks-book-life)
- He rejected the "tourist version of a culture" ("I am Mexico!"), said "Mexico is way more complicated than anybody thinks," and made male designs deliberately busy so the eye goes to faces. Baroque colour alternates with desaturated passages. — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=107509)

**Pixar's Coco (2017)**
- Over three years the team made research trips to Mexico City, Oaxaca and Guanajuato, including Xochimilco and family stays in Tlacolula and other towns. Guanajuato's layered tunnels and Oaxaca's stacked buildings shaped the Land of the Dead, and the Monte Albán ruins were also an inspiration. — [/Film, World of Coco](https://www.slashfilm.com/552987/world-of-coco/); [/Film location visit with Adrian Molina](https://www.slashfilm.com/556568/coco-locations/)
- The towers show history by height, with pre-Columbian forms at the base, colonial in the middle and modern on top, because "people are always dying so the land is always growing" (sets supervisor Chris Bernardi). DP Danielle Feinberg lit an always-night city using cemetery candles, festival lights and street-vendor bulbs. The team studied *Spirited Away* and *Howl's Moving Castle*. About 7 million lights were used. — [/Film, World of Coco](https://www.slashfilm.com/552987/world-of-coco/)
- Skeleton rules: Unkrich gave skulls large emotive eyes ("without them they're just blank slates"), with eye sockets that stretch to act as brows. This departs from folk calaveras' black hollow eyes. Faces carry painted Day of the Dead designs. The motto was "embrace the skeleton," so bones should be felt under clothing. Alebrijes were invented by Pedro Linares in the 1930s. — [/Film, Bringing skeletons to life in Coco](https://www.slashfilm.com/skeletons-in-coco-pixar); [ABC11, Coco's visual language](https://abc11.com/entertainment/the-colorful-symbols-of-pixars-coco/2524370/)

**Blasphemous (The Game Kitchen, Seville)**
- The look comes from Seville's Holy Week, iconography and folklore; the hero's helmet is based on penitents' capirotes. Enrique Cabeza: "Seville is full of religious images… here in southern Spain all that religious art is really more popular culture than religion… within our cultural DNA." Painter references include Murillo, Goya (notably *A Procession of Flagellants*, 1812-19), Ribera, Velázquez and Zurbarán. — [Heavy Mag interview](https://heavymag.com.au/interview-blasphemous-the-game-kitchen/); [Frieze, Thomas McMullan, 7 Oct 2019](https://frieze.com/article/how-video-game-blasphemous-embraces-catholic-gothic-tradition) (Frieze page blocked; content via search summary); [Epic Games Store feature on Blasphemous 2's cultural influences](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-language-barriers-and-cultural-influences-led-to-the-creation-of-blasphemous-2)

**Thief: The Dark Project's City (Looking Glass, 1998)**
- The City combines late-medieval society with early-industrial technology and magic in a dense town: half-timbered houses lit by electric streetlamps. The Hammerites (a religion of the Builder) carry the mechanical-religious tension. The game began as "Dark Camelot." — [SF Crowsnest steampunk field guide](https://www.sfcrowsnest.info/steampunk-field-guide/entries/thief-the-dark-project.html); [Thief Wiki, Dark Camelot](https://thief.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Camelot)

**Pentiment and Disco Elysium (investigation and dialogue)**
- Pentiment uses six custom fonts by Lettermatic to mark class and education: peasant script, middle-class cursive, Humanist script for the university-educated, Gothic blackletter for old monks, and print for printers. More than 2,700 glyphs were drawn, partly hand-inked. A character's font changes as Andreas learns who they are; Baron Lorenz shifts to Andreas's own hand once revealed as his intellectual equal. Sawyer: "the more we used fonts to show how characters changed… the more people picked up on it." — [PCGamesN, Pentiment's art, fonts and story](https://www.pcgamesn.com/pentiment/art-fonts-story); [Lettermatic case study](https://lettermatic.com/custom/pentiment); [GameBanshee, Sawyer on fonts](https://www.gamebanshee.com/96xr8)
- Sawyer's historical grounding: he studied early-modern witch-hunting in the Holy Roman Empire, and the printing press and Reformation form the backdrop of Tassing. He also gave a 2024 Vienna lecture, "Painting on Silicon." — [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/features/exploring-protestant-reformation-history-in-pentiment); [University of Vienna library event](https://bibliothek.univie.ac.at/events/007867.html)
- Disco Elysium: GDC talk "'Disco Elysium': Meaningless Choices and Impractical Advice" by Justin Keenan (ZA/UM) on branching-dialogue practice. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027048/-Disco-Elysium-Meaningless-Choices). The Thought Cabinet stores internalised ideas that act as perks and traits and unlock dialogue: 53 thoughts, about 16 per run. Robert Kurvitz called it a "quagmire" that almost sank the project. — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/how-the-feature-that-almost-sunk-disco-elysium-was/1100-6472676/); [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/39hc9)

**Kentucky Route Zero (Cardboard Computer)**
- Three people over seven years made a magical-realist game in the line of García Márquez, Lynch and Flannery O'Connor. The surreal is treated as ordinary; for example, a floor of bears among office clerks that nobody remarks on. "The lack of commentary IS the commentary." — [GamingTrend editorial](https://gamingtrend.com/feature/editorials/magical-realism-video-games-and-why-kentucky-route-zero-matters); [NPR, Reading the Game](https://www.npr.org/2021/02/11/966499158/reading-the-game-kentucky-route-zero)

**Mike Mignola's Hellboy in Mexico**
- In *Hellboy in Mexico* (2010, Mignola with Richard Corben; collected with Mick McMahon, Fábio Moon and Gabriel Bá), Hellboy joins three luchador brothers who slay vampires, fights Aztec gods and a brujo, and wrestles Frankenstein-style monsters. It mixes Mexican mythology, lucha libre and pulp monster movies. — [Mass Movement review](https://massmovement.co.uk/hellboy-in-mexico-mike-mignola-richard-corben-mick-mcmahon-fabio-moon-gabriel-ba-dave-stewart-dark-horse-books/); [Uproxx, Corben on sending Hellboy to Mexico](https://uproxx.com/?p=1565996)

**Silvia Moreno-Garcia, Mexican Gothic (2020)**
- The novel is set in the 1950s and based on Real del Monte, Hidalgo, a silver town nicknamed "Little Cornwall" after its 19th-century British mining operations. Its English cemetery looked to the author "like something out of a Hammer film." She was interested in what happens when foreigners arrive to exploit local resources and people. — [ABA/Bookweb Q&A](https://bookweb.org/node/577738); [Random House book club kit (PDF)](https://www.randomhousebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/mexican-gothic-by-silvia-moreno-garcia-book-club-kit.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Gothic)

### Inferences
- **Del Toro's colour memo can become a project document.** Assign one meaning-colour per faction or supernatural force, for example the amber of time and ghosts, cempasúchil orange for the dead, cyan for the colonial or foreign. Forbid those colours everywhere else. With a PS2-era palette this is cheap and very readable.
- **"Eye protein" maps directly to environmental storytelling.** Each room or set makes one statement that a stealthy player can read in one glance.
- **The Gutiérrez handmade surface is a strong fit for a stylized PS2 look.** Painted wood, chipped paint, papier-mâché and cartonería (Judas figures, alebrijes) suit low-poly forms with hand-painted textures far better than realism does. It also connects directly to Grim Fandango's papier-mâché calacas.
- **Coco's history-by-height idea** fits CDMX almost literally. Mexico City sits on Tenochtitlan, so a vertical cross-section of Aztec under colonial under Porfirian/1920s levels gives a ready "underworld" layer for stealth (sunken churches, drainage tunnels, buried templo layers).
- **Anachronism to exploit:** alebrijes appeared in the 1930s, just after the setting's period, so an alternate-1920s world can make them earlier, as a "big lie."
- **Blasphemous's point that religion is popular culture** holds for Mexico (processions, ex-votos, Santa Muerte-like folk devotion, retablos). Treat it as everyday texture, not set dressing.
- **Pentiment fonts and Disco Elysium's internal voices** offer cheap, writer-driven ways to show class and character in a dialogue-heavy investigation game.
- **Mexican Gothic's foreign extraction** (British mine owners in Hidalgo) supplies a historically real gothic antagonist type for the 1920s: foreign oil, mining and railway interests in post-Revolution Mexico.

### Gaps
- Del Toro's films and Mignola's Mexican stories were covered through interviews and reviews, not the original art books (for example *Guillermo del Toro: Cabinet of Curiosities*), which I did not reach.
- I found no direct Cardboard Computer interview on magical-realist method; the sources are editorials.
- Thief's City has no developer interview here; the sources are wikis and secondary guides.
- I found no source on Coco's or The Book of Life's specific palette swatches.

## Practical lessons for a small team: compact dense worlds and the "3-5 big lies"

### Takeaway
The case studies agree on a pattern. Ground the world in one real place and period studied first-hand; change it with a small number of large, internally consistent rules (one economy, one rupture, one cosmology); give each district a job, a class, a palette and a creature or prop family; and make a small space feel big through density, verticality, layered routes, landmarks and an unseen but fully written wider world. I found no single published source that names a "3-5 big lies" rule; it is a synthesis of these examples.

### Cited Findings
- Arkane wrote a whole continent's backstory but built only what the player sees in detail. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Dishonored's levels are "dense," with many routes, heavy verticality (rooftops, windows) and landmarks so players know where they have been. — [GameBanshee review](https://www.gamebanshee.com/x886); [Ulster student paper (PDF)](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/b00860009-fad/files/2023/02/Level-Design-Techniques-in-Dishonored-2.pdf)
- Karnaca's districts are divided by a single geographic and economic logic (a mountain, silver dust, windbreakers). — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/article/23269-dishonored-2-sebastien-mitton-art-director-arkane-studios-interview)
- Grim Fandango splits a compact game into four acts, each in one distinct location with its own industry. — [Wikipedia, Grim Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Fandango)
- Deadlock's single rupture (the Maelstrom) normalises the occult across the whole city. Its map splits into class-coded halves (Uptown and Downtown), reportedly reskinned into themed districts (city centre, haunted university, stock exchange). — [Deadlock Wiki, Storyline](https://deadlock.wiki/Storyline); [Deadlock Wiki, Hidden King](https://deadlock.wiki/Hidden_King); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/moba/deadlocks-gigantic-new-update-gives-its-city-a-spooky-new-coat-of-paint-and-debuts-6-new-heroes-including-the-one-and-only-rat-king/) (search summary)
- Coco's sets supervisor: research trips "are a lot for inspiration. For me, sets are about a sense of place." — [Minnesota Daily, Designing the Land of the Dead](https://mndaily.com/215240/arts-entertainment/aecoco) (search summary)
- Kentucky Route Zero was made by three people over seven years; its scope came from writing and mood, not size. — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/feature/editorials/magical-realism-video-games-and-why-kentucky-route-zero-matters)

### Inferences
**Candidate "big lies" for alternate 1920s Mexico City.** These are suggestions modelled on whale oil, the Maelstrom and the Number Nine:
1. **One invented resource or tech.** Example: an occult fuel or ink refined from something native, such as copal, cempasúchil, axolotl or lake silt, powering lamps, trams and wards. Like whale oil, it should explain the lighting, which matters for a stealth game.
2. **One historical rupture.** Example: a point during the Revolution or the 1920s Cristero tensions when the dead or the old gods became publicly real, as the Maelstrom does in Deadlock. Everything else stays as the 1920s really were: Obregón and Calles politics, tranvías, cabarets, Estridentistas, muralists, Art Deco and Neocolonial building.
3. **One cosmology.** An Aztec, Catholic and folk underworld, such as Mictlán's nine levels, that is literally under the city, using Coco's vertical layering and Grim's journey.
4. **Optionally one or two institutions twisted:** a Department-of-Death-style bureaucracy (Grim), a Patron-like pair of factions mapped onto real class geography (Deadlock), or foreign mining or oil interests as gothic antagonists (*Mexican Gothic*).

Keep everything else grounded in photo research: azoteas, vecindad courtyards, pulquerías, trajineras, tezontle and cantera stone, and 1920s signage and typography.

**Making a compact open world feel big:**
- **Districts with jobs.** Give each district an industry, a class, a palette colour, a supernatural "creature family" and a landmark visible from elsewhere (Grim's four years, Karnaca's mountain, Deadlock's themed lanes).
- **Verticality and layered routes.** Use streets, rooftops and azoteas, and a below-ground layer of canals, drainage and Aztec ruins. Three layers triple the play space on the same footprint.
- **The unseen world written in full.** Fully write the rest of the country and city in documents, radio, newspapers and dialogue (Arkane's continent, Deadlock's New York Oracle) but build only a few blocks.
- **Landmarks and silhouettes for orientation and stealth reading.** Distinct towers, domes, an Ángel-like column and volcano silhouettes on the skybox.
- **Del Toro's colour memo** and **Pentiment-style typographic signals** as low-cost tools that do not rely on graphics.
- **Puzzle and dialogue dependency charts per district** (Grim's 1996 document) so density comes from interlocking content, not area.

### Gaps
- No published source sets out a formal "3-5 big lies" doctrine for game worldbuilding. The list above is my synthesis from the case studies.
- I found no GDC talk specifically on making small open worlds feel large in these titles. Arkane level-design talks (for example on Dishonored 2's Clockwork Mansion) were not located in this pass and would be worth a follow-up search on GDC Vault.
