# The Underground's supernatural: references, and what we take from each

Oct 17 2026. The user's picks to build first: the ánimas in the drain, the nahual on the bench,
the dark train. The user asked that each mix Mike Mignola and Deadlock into our own setting.
Every strange thing keeps a cause in the world's events (world-built-by-its-events); none is
explained on screen.

## What we take, by source

**Mignola and Dave Stewart.**
- Space and black do the work. The supernatural reads as a shape, a silhouette in solid black,
  or a flat pale form with a hard edge, never a soft realistic glow.
- One accent colour per thing, laid flat, its depth coming from flat colour rather than
  rendering.
- Reviewers note how he makes a few feet feel abyssal: the uncanny keeps its distance.
- *Hellboy in Mexico* (1956, luchadores, Aztec mummies, a brujo who steals a dead man) shows him
  taking folklore as it works best and bending it to his story. That is our licence too.

**Deadlock (Valve, "City Never Sleeps", Sept 29 2026).**
- Its neutral creatures became Haunts: spirits living *inside* the city's ordinary things.
- Examples: lanterns holding souls, discarded dark thoughts as trash bags, barrels that are
  mimics, stagehands that are marionettes worked by ghostly hands, spectral library things.
- Souls are glowing orbs, and urns trap rogue spirits.
- The rule we take: the uncanny wears the station's own furniture and lives in its everyday
  objects. It does not arrive as monsters from outside.

**Mexico City's Metro and folklore.**
- *Pino Suárez* is the next stop from the Zócalo on Line 2. Its real temple to Ehécatl, the wind
  god (a form of Quetzalcóatl), was found while the station was dug in 1968 and stands in the
  passage between Lines 1 and 2 (INAH).
- Its legend: a track worker killed by a car years ago still walks the tunnels, asking after
  the orange train (Alex Lora's song "El fantasma"). Chilango notes an editor likely invented
  it, which is fine for us.
- A recent passenger's account: trains passing completely empty, one after another, through a
  station that should have been full.
- *El nahual* (UNAM; Milenio): an enormous black dog with red eyes that frightens people at
  night and drives them mad. In the old telling it is an old, unkempt man with round red eyes
  who becomes a dog to run the fields doing harm.
- *Las ánimas*: the souls in purgatory, prayed for with candles. The dead are fed on their days,
  and the marigold trail leads them home (as hers does, to the crack).

## The three pieces, mixed

**The ánimas in the drain.**
- **Cause:** the drain runs down to the old city's cistern, where the drowned of the Conquest's
  siege lie unprayed-for. They are drawn up by her candles and the trail of marigolds.
- **Mignola:** small flat pale flames with a crisp edge and a dark core, the shape of a
  candle's tongue, not a fuzzy orb. Their light on the grate's bars from below is the image:
  black bars cut against a cold glow.
- **Deadlock:** souls as small bright shapes that slip away from the near; spirits in things.
  When one rises, it goes where the living light candles: it leans toward her stall's flames.
- **Colour:** the seeped green of our colour rule (Vfx.SEEPED), paler at the core.

**The nahual on the bench.**
- **Cause:** the old man who sits on the bench every night (his newspaper is left there, his hat
  beside it) is the nahual.
- **What you see:** you rarely see him, only the dog: an enormous black dog lying under or
  beside the bench, all one Mignola black shape with two round red eyes and nothing drawn
  inside it.
- **Behaviour:** it watches you; it is gone when you look away and back.
- **Deadlock:** the uncanny tied to an ordinary object, here the bench, the hat, the paper.
  Never a fight.

**The dark train.**
- **Cause:** Ehécatl's wind. The line was dug through the wind god's temple, and rarely a train
  runs that the Company does not run: an orange car with no light in it.
- **What you feel:** its sound is only wind, no wheels. Every flame in the corridor leans and
  gutters, the bulb swings, petals and paper drift along the floor.
- **What you see:** a dead track worker's lamp is seen once at the tunnel's mouth.
- **Mignola:** the car as a flat dark silhouette, its windows black.
- **Deadlock:** the subway as a haunted lane. In the bay it is felt rather than seen (there is no
  track here yet); the full sight waits for the station and the ride (Part 2).
